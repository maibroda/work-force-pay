## Not yet merged (branch `continuous-feedback`, one migration: 20261130090000_continuous_feedback)

- **Performance management, step (c): continuous feedback** (the founder's go of 2026-11-07; plan: `docs/plans/continuous-feedback-plan.md`). A **feedback entry** is a short, dated note about one employee, of two kinds: **Recognition** (something done well) and **Coaching** (something to improve, and what would help), written by the employee's **reporting manager** or by **HR**.
- **The employee always sees it.** There are **no private notes about a person's performance**: **My feedback** (My HR) shows everything recorded about them and who wrote it, and they may add **one reply** to each entry, which the manager and HR can read.
- **Who may write about whom, enforced on the server:** HR about anyone; anyone else who gives feedback (supervisors and operations managers) only about **their own direct reports** (the people who name them as reporting manager); **nobody about themselves**, HR included. For anyone a manager may not write about, the answer is only "not found". Only a person who is still active can be written about; the date it relates to cannot be in the future.
- **Never edited.** HR **voids** an entry made in error **with a reason**; it is kept, marked, and disappears from the employee's page and the default lists. A manager reads only their direct reports' entries (**My team's feedback**); HR reads everyone's with filters (**Feedback**, under Performance management).
- **Evidence on an appraisal, never a score.** The appraisal page shows the reviewer (the employee's manager) and HR a read-only **Feedback in this period** panel of entries dated inside the cycle's period. It **changes no rating or recommendation**; the employee does not see this panel (they read their own entries under My feedback). Feedback changes no pay, status or discipline.
- **Permissions (new):** `feedback.give` (HR administrator, company administrator, super administrator, supervisor, operations: each limited to their own direct reports unless they also hold manage) and `feedback.manage` (HR administrator and the administrators: read everyone's and void). **The auditor does not get feedback**, nor do finance and payroll roles.
- **Personal data:** a person's entries and their replies are part of the **copy of their data** they can ask for (never an entry voided as a mistake), and when an employee is **erased** the words in every entry, reply and void reason are removed (the shape stays: that an entry existed, its kind and date; the audit log is untouched). **Audit:** every step by ids only, never what a note, reply or reason says.
- **Migration (reversible):** one enum and one new table. Round-tripped (up, down, up) on a copy of the seeded database: employees unchanged, schema identical. Explain-back note: `docs/plans/continuous-feedback-explain-back.md`.
- Tests (written first): `tests/unit/feedback.test.ts` (8), `tests/integration/feedback.test.ts` (17, including the copy of their data and the erasure); the navigation tests list the three new pages. **Not built (listed in the plan):** peer and client feedback, requests for feedback, notifications, links to a KPI or criterion, and any analytics.

## Merged in #161 (one migration: 20261127090000_document_workflow_and_task_links)

- **Electronic employee documents, part B: the approval workflow and the onboarding and exit links (the founder, 2026-11-07).** A document that needs approval now goes **Pending (uploaded) to Submitted to Under review to Approved or Rejected**. Someone who manages documents **submits** it; an approver **takes it for review** and **approves or rejects** it. **A rejection needs a reason**, and the reviewer, the date and the note are recorded and shown on the register. Nothing is approved merely by being uploaded. **A replacement starts the workflow over** (a changed file must be approved again).
- **The person who uploaded or submitted a document can never review it** (enforced on the server). A reviewer who has taken a document is the only one who can decide it. A new permission, **`document.approve`**, is held by the **HR administrator, company administrator and super administrator**.
- **Checklist steps can follow a document.** A step in the onboarding or exit checklist template can name a **document type** (Settings > Onboarding & exit checklists; one button, **Link the standard steps**, links the clear matches: Offer letter signed, Employment contract signed & filed, Medical examination, Guard / firearms license verified; for exits Handover of duties, Exit interview conducted, Clearance sign-off, Return of company property / equipment). **New hires and new exits then get tasks that follow their document**: **Pending** (no file, or not yet submitted), **Submitted**, **Under review**, and **Completed only when the document is approved**, recorded as done by the approval. A rejection leaves the step Pending with the reason; replacing an approved document reopens the step.
- **Upload from the step itself** (the profile's Lifecycle tab, the HR onboarding tracker and the exit page): the step names the type, so there is no type to choose. A type that **needs no approval completes the step on upload**, recorded "no approval required". **A step linked to a document cannot be ticked by hand**: upload the document, or mark the step **not applicable with a reason** (as before). Steps with no document type are completed by hand exactly as before, with the same audit trail.
- **Existing tasks are not changed:** only steps made from now on follow a document. Broad steps such as "Documents collected (ID, certificates, references)" stay done by hand until you split them into steps of their own. Every submission, review start, approval, rejection and task completion or reopening is **audited by ids** (a rejection's reason is recorded, never a file name).
- **Migration (reversible):** eight additive columns on the document table (who submitted, who reviewed, when, the note), a document type on checklist template items, and a document type and document on onboarding and exit tasks. Round-tripped (up, down, up) on a copy of the seeded database with 2,399 onboarding tasks, 108 exit tasks and 31 documents unchanged. Explain-back note: `docs/plans/employee-documents-workflow-explain-back.md`.
- Tests (written first): `tests/unit/documents-workflow.test.ts` (9), `tests/integration/documents-workflow.test.ts` (19). **Not built yet (the last pull request):** the central HR register with missing and expiring tracking and the approval queue, and the employee's own read-only view. There is still **no virus scanning**.

## Merged in #160 (one migration: 20261126090000_employee_documents)

- **Electronic employee documents, part A: the document store (the founder, 2026-11-07; plan: `docs/plans/employee-documents.md`).** The Documents tab of an employee's profile now has a **document register** and an **upload panel**. Until now it held only a typed reference to a paper or scanned file; no file was kept anywhere.
- **Document types are data.** Seven fixed categories (employment, identification, educational and professional, compliance, medical, statutory, exit) with the standard types you listed seeded for each company. **HR > HR setup > Document types** lets an authorised person add a type, and switch a type on or off, or change whether it needs approval, has an expiry date or is sensitive, **without any change to the application**.
- **Upload:** drag and drop or choose files (PDF, DOC, DOCX, JPG, JPEG, PNG), up to 10 MB each and 10 at a time, with a progress bar and a plain success or refusal for each file; one refused file never holds up the others. Each file becomes its own document, with its type, optional reference number, issue date, expiry date (for types that have one) and description. **The server checks every file again**: the extension, the size, and the file's own first bytes must agree, so a program renamed as a PDF is refused.
- **Files are stored encrypted** (AES-256-GCM) in the database, in their own table, so listing documents never loads file bytes. **A replacement is a new version; the earlier ones are kept** and can be viewed and downloaded, with who replaced them and when. Nothing is ever deleted: a document is **archived**.
- **Opening a file** is only through a **signed link that works for five minutes**, for one signed-in user, issued after the permission check and checked again when used; PDFs and images preview in the browser, Word files download. Every link, view, download, upload, replacement and archive is **recorded in the audit log by ids** (never a file name or description).
- **Access:** four new permissions. `document.view` (the register and the non-sensitive files), `document.manage` (upload, replace, archive), `document.sensitive` (open identification and medical files, and see them listed) and `document.configure` (document types). **HR administrator, company administrator and super administrator** hold all four; the **auditor** holds view only. **Finance and the other roles no longer see the document register** (they could read the old reference list through general HR access). A document a person may not open is not listed and is "not found" if asked for by its address.
- **Records from before are kept:** the old reference-only records are not changed or deleted. They show as **"Reference only (before document management)"**, with no approval invented for them, and a file can be attached to one to turn it into a normal document. The old table on the tab now shows only those records, and hides the identification and medical kinds from people without sensitive access.
- **Other places that read documents** follow the same rules: the HR compliance report names the type and leaves out archived and (for those without access) sensitive documents; the expiry digest and the overview count ignore archived documents and name the type. **When a person's data is erased, their stored file bytes are deleted too.**
- **Configuration needed:** a host environment variable **`DOCUMENT_ENCRYPTION_KEY`** (32 random bytes, base64; see `.env.example`). **Back it up separately from the database: a lost key means the stored files cannot be read.** Uploads and downloads are refused until it is set. A request to the upload route is limited to about 12 MB (`middlewareClientMaxBodySize` in `next.config.ts`); the screen sends one file per request.
- **Migration (reversible):** two enums, three tables (document types, versions, the encrypted bytes) and six additive columns on the existing document table. Round-tripped (up, down, up) on a copy of the seeded database, with an existing document row proved to survive. **The down script destroys uploaded files, and says so.** Explain-back note: `docs/plans/employee-documents-explain-back.md`.
- Tests (written first): `tests/unit/documents.test.ts` (16), `tests/integration/documents.test.ts` (25). **Not built yet (later pull requests):** the approval workflow (submit, review, approve, reject with a reason), the onboarding and exit checklist links, the central HR register with missing and expiring tracking, and the employee's own read-only view. **No virus scanning** is included (recommended before production use).

## Merged in #157 (one migration: 20261125090000_performance_kpis)

- **Job descriptions and KPIs now feed the appraisal (the founder, 2026-11-06; plan: `docs/plans/performance-management-phase1.md`).** Each employee category can have a **job description** (title, purpose, responsibilities) with **KPIs**: a unit, a direction (higher or lower is better), an exact **target** and a **weight**. Screen: **HR > Performance management > Job descriptions & KPIs**.
- **A second person makes it active.** HR drafts; someone else (`hr.approve`, never the drafter) makes it active. An active version is never edited: a change is a new draft that starts as a copy, and making it active retires the old version. A review already under way keeps the KPIs it started with.
- **A cycle can give KPIs a share of the overall score.** Launching a cycle has a new **KPI share (%)**, **0 by default, which scores exactly as appraisals did before**. With a share, the reviewer enters each KPI's **actual result**; the system works out the achievement (a percentage of the target, whole-number arithmetic, rounded half up) and a **1 to 5 score from the company's bands**; the overall score blends the criteria score and the KPI score. Staff whose category has no KPIs are left out of a KPI-weighted cycle and listed with the reason.
- **The bands are settings, not code.** Defaults: below 50% = 1; 50% to under 75% = 2; 75% to under 100% = 3; 100% to under 120% = 4; 120% and over = 5. A user with HR-configure access can replace them on the same screen; they apply to reviews submitted from then on.
- **The employee sees no KPI result or score until the appraisal is signed off**, like the ratings today. Changes are audited by ids and field names, never the text of a description or a target.
- **Migration (reversible):** three tables, three enums and five additive columns (the cycle's KPI share, the bands on the HR policy, and three score columns on the appraisal). Round-tripped (down, up) on a copy of the seeded database. **No existing appraisal changes.** Explain-back note with a worked example: `docs/plans/performance-kpis-explain-back.md`.
- Tests (written first): `tests/unit/performance.test.ts` (23), `tests/integration/performance-kpis.test.ts` (17). **Not built:** the PIP workflow, continuous feedback, the probation review ending in a confirmation decision and analytics (the rest of the performance-management order); per-employee or per-site targets.

## Merged in #153 (one migration: 20261124090000_paid_days_basis_and_unpaid_leave)

- **The paid-days basis agreed with each client, set on the contract (the founder, 2026-11-05).** A month's pay can now be divided by the **days in the month** (calendar) or by the **working days** in it (for example 22, Monday to Friday by default, or the contract's own 5, 6 or 7-day week), optionally **without the confirmed public holidays**. **On the working-days basis a weekend or holiday day adds no basic pay** (work on it is paid as overtime through the existing route), so pay on that basis can never exceed one full gross. **A contract with no setting keeps the company's payroll rule exactly as before.** Screen: **Clients & contracts > Paid days**.
- **A change is proposed and approved by a second person** (the payroll manager; the same maker and checker rule as personal pay rates), takes effect from the **first day of a month** (the basis at the start of a pay month applies to the whole month), is effective-dated and keeps its history. **A pending proposal never changes pay.**
- **Public holidays:** the company enters its holidays each year and a payroll manager **confirms the year** (**Payroll > Pay setup > Public holidays**); no date is in the code. A contract that excludes holidays cannot be paid cleanly for a month in an unconfirmed year: the pay run raises a **critical finding** until the year is confirmed. A person who worked on contracts with different bases in a month is **flagged for review** and paid on the company's rule.
- **Unpaid leave:** switch it on in the leave policy ("Allow unpaid leave", off by default). An employee (or HR) can ask for unpaid leave; it needs no annual entitlement and **does not use up annual leave**. Once approved, **its working days are not paid days** (pay is reduced as for an absence; pension, PAYE and NHF follow the reduced pay), for staff on the work register and for staff on a personal pay rate.
- **Migration (reversible):** three tables (the basis, the holidays, the yearly confirmation), an enum, and two additive columns on leave. Round-tripped (down, up) on a copy of the test database. **No existing contract's pay changes.** Explain-back note with a worked example: `docs/plans/paid-days-explain-back.md`.
- Tests: `tests/unit/paid-days.test.ts` (12), `tests/unit/engine-paid-days.test.ts` (9), `tests/integration/paid-days.test.ts` (11), written first. Not yet built: the payslip does not yet list unpaid days.

The app works out **what that guard costs the company** and shows the margin before the quote goes out. **It does this by running the real payroll engine** on a synthetic full month for one guard (nothing in payroll changes and nothing is saved there), so the employer's pension (10% of the pensionable pay: Basic, Housing and Transport in the standard structure), ITF and NSITF (1% of gross each) and the other employer costs come out exactly as payroll works them, at the percentages in the **effective-dated rules in force on the quote date**; no percentage is written into the code. The guard's gross is the bill rate times the **share paid to the guard (70% unless the line carries its own)**; cost is that gross plus the employer costs; margin is the bill minus the cost, held to the kobo, as a percentage to two decimals. **Each line saves its figures and the rule versions it used, so a later rule change never alters a quote.** **Billed guards and the people the shifts need are separate** (founder, 2026-10-29): each line has the **guards the client is billed for** (revenue = bill rate x these) and the **personnel needed to cover the agreed shifts** (cost = what one person costs x these; blank means the same as the billed number). A 24/7 post that bills 12 guards but needs 14 people shows its true margin, and the client is shown only the billed guards. **The minimum gross margin starts at 15% for a new organization** (a share of the bill; the company administrator can change it, and it is audited). **A quote below the minimum cannot be sent to the client until a formally designated approver, who did not make the quote, approves it with a reason**; the company administrator designates approvers (active sales managers only) under Sales Settings, and with nobody designated no such quote can be sent. The check is made against the minimum in force **when the quote is sent**, and an approval counts only for the minimum it was given against, so raising the minimum means approving again. **A flag for the founder: with the 70:30 split and the starting employer-cost percentages, a standard quote's gross margin is about 9% of the bill, so it falls below 15% and needs approval; a 15% margin needs the guard's share to be about 54% or less, or lower employer-cost percentages. This is a business decision, not a defect.** A quote is a draft until it is sent; **after that its lines cannot be changed (the database refuses)** and a change is a **new version** (the earlier one is marked superseded). **Accepting a quote copies its monthly total into the deal's value**; only one quote of a deal can be accepted. **The Proposal sent stage now needs a quote that has been sent or accepted** (a draft does not count). **The client's PDF** shows the sites, posts, guards, the rate for each guard and the monthly totals, the validity and the notes, and **never the cost, margin or rules** (tested); every download is audited. The proposal PDF drawing code is shared with the bureau's proposals (their output is unchanged). Reversible migrations (`20261106120000_sales_quotes`: two tables, one type, one setting column, a guard trigger; `20261106130000_quote_staffing_approvers`: the personnel column, the 15% default for new organizations and the approver list). Tests: 15 unit and 24 integration.

## Merged: failed-login audit (#79, branch `failed-login-audit`)

- **The two-factor step can no longer be guessed without limit** (a change to sign-in, approved by the founder). Before, any number of wrong codes was accepted (nothing counted, locked or logged them), and a correct password reset the failure count, so wrong guesses could be mixed with right passwords and never reach the lock. Now **a wrong code counts on the same counter as a wrong password: five in total lock the account for 15 minutes**, and a locked account refuses even the right code. For an account with two-factor on, **a correct password no longer clears the count; only the whole sign-in succeeding does** (an account without two-factor is unchanged). The message for a wrong code is still "Incorrect code.". The count is raised in one database step, so many guesses sent at the same moment cannot read the same old number.
- **Failed sign-ins and related failures are now in the audit log** (rule 5), in the account's own organization, with the address and the browser, and never the password, code, reset token or sign-in challenge. New actions: `LOGIN_FAILED` (reasons `WRONG_PASSWORD`, `ACCOUNT_LOCKED`, `ACCOUNT_INACTIVE`), `TOTP_FAILED`, `PASSWORD_RESET_REQUESTED`, `PASSWORD_RESET_FAILED` (`TOKEN_USED`, `TOKEN_EXPIRED`), `PASSWORD_CHANGE_FAILED`, `TOTP_DISABLE_FAILED`; `ACCOUNT_LOCKED` now says whether passwords or codes caused it. Attempts on a locked or switched-off account are written at most once every five minutes. The audit report has a **Show: Security events** filter. An email with **no account** goes to the application log only, as a keyed hash plus the address (never the email, because people type passwords in the email box). The replies to the person signing in are unchanged. **Not done:** a limit by IP address (waits for the host decision); a limit on wrong current passwords or codes on the change-password and turn-off-two-factor screens. No pay figure changes; no migration; no new dependency.
