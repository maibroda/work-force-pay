## Merged in #89 on 2026-11-02 into its base branch, and in main with #83 on 2026-11-03 (branch `take-home-floor`, on top of #87)

- **The take-home floor** (the net-pay floor of `docs/plans/golden-key-rule-settings.md`). A new payroll-rule setting, **Take-home floor % of gross**, effective-dated like the rest of the rule. **0, the default, means no floor, so no existing payroll changes**: it is a client-specific policy (the founder's key calls it a "policy example"), not a statutory one. Above 0, **loan and salary-advance recoveries are limited so that net pay is not taken below that share of gross**; PAYE, pension, NHF and every other deduction (penalties, recoveries, other) come off first and are never reduced; recoveries are taken in the order given, each up to the room left, and the floor is rounded half up to the kobo once. **What is not taken is not lost**: each cut is flagged as a warning on the payroll, shown on the payslip line ("… deferred: take-home floor"), and **when the payroll is locked** the recovery is written back as what was really taken (audited as DEDUCTION_DEFERRED), so the loan book, which works its balance out from its instalments, still shows the rest as owed and the next month's schedule takes it. The app has no separate "cooperative" deduction, so the floor covers the two kinds that have a carry-forward (loans and salary advances); the key's cooperative deduction is entered as a loan in the golden test. Migration `20261105140000_take_home_floor` (one column, default 0; `down.sql` reverses it).

## Merged in #83 on 2026-11-03 (branch `paye-by-state`, including the audit seals of plan #82)

- **The audit log is now tamper-evident** (rule 5, plan #82). Every audit entry gets a **seal**: a SHA-256 hash of the entry's content, chained to the previous seal of the same organization by a keyed hash (HMAC) under a new secret, `AUDIT_SEAL_KEY`. A **sealing job** (`/api/cron/audit-seal`, every minute) seals entries a minute after they are written, so the audit write path is unchanged and payroll is not slowed. A **daily check** (`/api/cron/audit-verify`) reads every seal against its entry and names any entry that was **changed, removed, added out of order or back-dated**, any **seal** that was rewritten or taken out, and any entries left **unsealed for over ten minutes** (the job has stopped); a failure is written to the audit log (`AUDIT_INTEGRITY_FAILED`) and **emailed** to the people who can view the audit log, with kinds and numbers but never audit content. A **weekly email** (`/api/cron/audit-heads`) sends each organization's chain head to the same people: **keep those emails, they are the copy outside the system**. A new screen, **Settings → Audit Integrity**, shows what is sealed and the last runs and has a **Check now** button (the check is itself audited). The seals are append-only at the database (no switch). Existing entries are sealed at the first run and marked **sealed late**: before sealing began the log is only as trustworthy as it was. A deliberate change to old entries (for example masking account numbers in the field-encryption rollout) must go through `redactAuditRows`, which writes an `AUDIT_REDACTION` entry with a keyed proof for each changed entry; a bare edit, or an inserted fake declaration, is still found. **This detects tampering, it does not prevent it; someone who holds both the database and `AUDIT_SEAL_KEY` could rebuild the chain**, which is what the weekly emails and keeping the key off the database server are for. Launch checklist: a new item (key set, sealing running, daily check clean). Measured on the test database: sealing 100,000 entries 120 s (a one-time cost), checking 100,000 entries 28 s. Migration `20261105110000_audit_seals` (a new table and its trigger; `down.sql` reverses it). New secret: `AUDIT_SEAL_KEY` (see `.env.example`, `docs/going-live.md`).

- **PAYE goes to each worker's state of residence** (plan #81, stage 1). Every employee has a **State of residence** (one of the 36 states or FCT; however it is typed, "ogun" or "Abuja", it is stored under its proper name; the employee form, the employee import and the employee page carry it). When a payroll is calculated the state is **copied onto each payroll record**, so a locked payroll never changes if the worker later moves. The PAYE remittance is now **one line per state** ("Lagos Internal Revenue Service (PAYE)") instead of one line for everyone, as pension is already split per fund administrator; the lines add up to the run's PAYE exactly (in whole kobo) and their head counts to the people taxed. A worker who pays PAYE and has **no recognised state** gets a **warning** on the payroll ("State not recorded"; nothing is blocked) and goes on a "State not recorded" line, so no PAYE is ever left out. The PAYE page shows PAYE by state and the state of each worker. **No pay figure changes.** The seeded and demo staff were given synthetic states (the demo client's staff live in Lagos, FCT and Rivers); the ThreeRoots acceptance test now expects those three PAYE lines instead of one. Migration `20261105090000_paye_state` (two nullable columns; `down.sql` reverses it).
- **PAYE filing dates and the late penalty are tax-rule data** (plan #81, stage 2): a PAYE due day (of the month after the pay month), an annual-return month and day, and a late-penalty percentage, all **blank until the tax adviser confirms them: no value is built in**. They are set when a PAYE rule version is created, travel with it to clients through the rule library, and are part of what HR and Finance sign: they join the rule's fingerprint **only when set**, so every rule signed before they existed keeps its fingerprint and stays verified (tested). The remittance page shows each PAYE line's **due date, days late and penalty** from the rule the payroll was calculated under; with no due day it says "not set in the tax rule" and nothing is ever overdue. Interest is not calculated. Migration `20261105100000_tax_rule_filing_dates` (four nullable columns; `down.sql` reverses it).
- **No two versions of a rule in force on the same days** (plan #81, stage 3, narrower than first planned): creating a PAYE version, and publishing a PAYE, pension or employer-contribution version to a client, is now refused with a plain message when it would share a day with a version that has already ended. (A version still open at that time was already refused or ended the day before; only ended ranges slipped through.) Service-level only; no database constraint, so no new database extension.

## Merged: failed-login audit (#79, branch `failed-login-audit`)

- **The two-factor step can no longer be guessed without limit** (a change to sign-in, approved by the founder). Before, any number of wrong codes was accepted (nothing counted, locked or logged them), and a correct password reset the failure count, so wrong guesses could be mixed with right passwords and never reach the lock. Now **a wrong code counts on the same counter as a wrong password: five in total lock the account for 15 minutes**, and a locked account refuses even the right code. For an account with two-factor on, **a correct password no longer clears the count; only the whole sign-in succeeding does** (an account without two-factor is unchanged). The message for a wrong code is still "Incorrect code.". The count is raised in one database step, so many guesses sent at the same moment cannot read the same old number.
- **Failed sign-ins and related failures are now in the audit log** (rule 5), in the account's own organization, with the address and the browser, and never the password, code, reset token or sign-in challenge. New actions: `LOGIN_FAILED` (reasons `WRONG_PASSWORD`, `ACCOUNT_LOCKED`, `ACCOUNT_INACTIVE`), `TOTP_FAILED`, `PASSWORD_RESET_REQUESTED`, `PASSWORD_RESET_FAILED` (`TOKEN_USED`, `TOKEN_EXPIRED`), `PASSWORD_CHANGE_FAILED`, `TOTP_DISABLE_FAILED`; `ACCOUNT_LOCKED` now says whether passwords or codes caused it. Attempts on a locked or switched-off account are written at most once every five minutes. The audit report has a **Show: Security events** filter. An email with **no account** goes to the application log only, as a keyed hash plus the address (never the email, because people type passwords in the email box). The replies to the person signing in are unchanged. **Not done:** a limit by IP address (waits for the host decision); a limit on wrong current passwords or codes on the change-password and turn-off-two-factor screens. No pay figure changes; no migration; no new dependency.

## Merged in #77 on 2026-10-27 (branch `verified-settings-gate`, now #77)

- **Payroll is blocked while a required statutory rule is unverified** (rule 3). For a **production client** (a client of the bureau whose engagement is **ACTIVE**), the PAYE, pension and employer-contribution rules in force for the period must each be exactly what **HR and Finance both signed** in the bureau's Statutory Rule Library, or the payroll is stopped. The check is made when a payroll is calculated, when a supplementary payroll is created, and when a payroll is **submitted, approved and locked**, so a rule changed after the calculation cannot slip through. It checks the rule's content against what was signed, so a rate changed directly in the database after sign-off is caught; how the rule reached the client (published, or copied when it was onboarded) does not matter. A rule the bureau is paying on that was **never put through the library** (a "baseline") counts as unverified, which matches the launch checklist. **There is no override**: the message names the rule and says to verify it in the library. **Every block is written to the audit log** (who tried, which step, which rule, why). Clients still being onboarded, the bureau's own organization and organizations that are not bureau clients are not stopped, but the payroll page now tells a person plainly which rules are not yet verified, and, when all are, who verified them and when. **No pay figure changes** (all 401 seeded figures are identical). The demo client now goes through the real propose, verify and publish steps and goes live, so it is gated and passes for the right reason. **Before the first real client goes ACTIVE, the bureau must run its current PAYE, pension and employer-contribution rules through the library once.** No migration.
