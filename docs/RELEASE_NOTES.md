## Not yet merged (branch `feature/crm-phase-5`, on top of #99, migration `20261106140000_sales_handover`)

- **Sales module, phase 5: the handover of an accepted quote to Operations** (plan `docs/crm/05-phase-5-plan.md`; founder's answers of 2026-10-29: a handshake between Operations and Sales, the contract made as a draft, the client's site contact kept for each site, and the Executive Director, Operations, or the Operations Manager to accept). **When the client accepts a quote, a draft handover (HO-00001...) is started automatically** with the sites and posts copied from the quote: what was billed, the people the shifts need, the shift and the bill rate (**never the company's cost or margin**, tested). It holds nothing in Operations yet. **Sales completes it**: the scope of service, the client's requirements, the start date and term, each site's address, state, local government, the **client's site contact** (name, role, phone stored as +234..., email), the **equipment** for the site, the **special service-level obligations**, and the employee category of each post (for the contract rate). "None" for equipment or service levels must be said explicitly. It cannot be sent while anything is missing, and the page lists what is. **The handshake:** Sales sends it; **a person in Operations who is formally designated (the Executive Director, Operations, or the Operations Manager; set by the company administrator under Sales Settings) and who did not prepare it either returns it with a question or accepts it.** A returned handover goes back to Sales, who changes it and sends it again; **Operations can only accept the version it was sent**. Who did what and when is kept in a history nobody can change. **Only on acceptance, all together or not at all, are made:** the client (or the one chosen; a second client of the same name is refused), a **draft contract** that Operations activates separately (term from the start date), **one contract rate for each employee category** (the quote's bill rate and the guard's share), and **a site for each site** with its approved strength set to **the people needed to cover the shifts**, not the guards billed. Nothing in payroll, pay rates, salary structures, billing or posted payroll is touched. **Once accepted nothing can change, the quote facts copied in never change, a sent handover's sites and posts cannot change, and the history cannot be altered: the database itself refuses.** A quote with a handover can no longer be revised. The existing client, contract, rate and site screens now share their record-making code with this (no change in what they do). Audit entries carry ids, numbers and counts only: no contact, phone, note or equipment appears. Reversible migration (five tables, two types, four guard triggers), round-tripped. New permission `sales.handover.accept` (Operations and the company administrator; it still needs the designation). **For the founder to confirm:** the contract's total value is left blank (the rates carry the prices); the same employee category cannot carry two bill rates in one handover. Tests: 10 unit and 21 integration.

## Not yet merged (branch `feature/crm-phase-3b`, on top of #98, migration `20261106120000_sales_quotes`)

- **Sales module, phase 3b: the cost-aware quote builder** (plan `docs/crm/03-phase-3-plan.md`). On a deal, **Make a quote**: each line is a site, a post, the number of guards the client pays for, a pay structure, and the **bill rate for each guard a month**. The app works out **what that guard costs the company** and shows the margin before the quote goes out. **It does this by running the real payroll engine** on a synthetic full month for one guard (nothing in payroll changes and nothing is saved there), so the employer's pension (10% of the pensionable pay: Basic, Housing and Transport in the standard structure), ITF and NSITF (1% of gross each) and the other employer costs come out exactly as payroll works them, at the percentages in the **effective-dated rules in force on the quote date**; no percentage is written into the code. The guard's gross is the bill rate times the **share paid to the guard (70% unless the line carries its own)**; cost is that gross plus the employer costs; margin is the bill minus the cost, held to the kobo, as a percentage to two decimals. **Each line saves its figures and the rule versions it used, so a later rule change never alters a quote.** **Billed guards and the people the shifts need are separate** (founder, 2026-10-29): each line has the **guards the client is billed for** (revenue = bill rate x these) and the **personnel needed to cover the agreed shifts** (cost = what one person costs x these; blank means the same as the billed number). A 24/7 post that bills 12 guards but needs 14 people shows its true margin, and the client is shown only the billed guards. **The minimum gross margin starts at 15% for a new organization** (a share of the bill; the company administrator can change it, and it is audited). **A quote below the minimum cannot be sent to the client until a formally designated approver, who did not make the quote, approves it with a reason**; the company administrator designates approvers (active sales managers only) under Sales Settings, and with nobody designated no such quote can be sent. The check is made against the minimum in force **when the quote is sent**, and an approval counts only for the minimum it was given against, so raising the minimum means approving again. **A flag for the founder: with the 70:30 split and the starting employer-cost percentages, a standard quote's gross margin is about 9% of the bill, so it falls below 15% and needs approval; a 15% margin needs the guard's share to be about 54% or less, or lower employer-cost percentages. This is a business decision, not a defect.** A quote is a draft until it is sent; **after that its lines cannot be changed (the database refuses)** and a change is a **new version** (the earlier one is marked superseded). **Accepting a quote copies its monthly total into the deal's value**; only one quote of a deal can be accepted. **The Proposal sent stage now needs a quote that has been sent or accepted** (a draft does not count). **The client's PDF** shows the sites, posts, guards, the rate for each guard and the monthly totals, the validity and the notes, and **never the cost, margin or rules** (tested); every download is audited. The proposal PDF drawing code is shared with the bureau's proposals (their output is unchanged). Reversible migrations (`20261106120000_sales_quotes`: two tables, one type, one setting column, a guard trigger; `20261106130000_quote_staffing_approvers`: the personnel column, the 15% default for new organizations and the approver list). Tests: 15 unit and 24 integration.

## Not yet merged (branch `feature/crm-phase-3a`, on top of #97, migration `20261106110000_sales_deal_board`)

- **Sales module, phase 3a: the deals board, stage rules and the forecast** (plan `docs/crm/03-phase-3-plan.md`; the cost-aware quote builder is phase 3b). **Board** (Sales > Deals Board): one column per stage with every deal as a card (value, probability, owner, days in stage, next step) and exact column totals of monthly value and weighted value; drag a deal to another stage or use the Move menu; a card turns red when the deal has had no activity for 14 days (a stage may set its own). **Every move asks for the next step**, writes a permanent stage-history row (with the days spent in the stage left) and an audit entry with stage names only. **A stage can require things first**: Site survey needs an expected start date and a contact; Won needs a monthly value above zero, a term and a start date; Lost needs a reason; the message names exactly what is missing and nothing is written when a move is refused. Won sets the probability to 100% and Lost to 0%, and **a won or lost deal is closed**: it can no longer be moved or edited. The owner may set the probability for an open stage (0 to 100). **The deal's weighted value is its monthly value times its probability over 100, rounded half up to the kobo once; a total is the exact sum of those**, and the yearly value is the monthly one times 12 and is never stored. **Forecast** by expected close month for open deals. A deal page shows where it stands, its figures, a change form and its stage history. A sales person works their own deals; a manager works everyone's. The "quote sent" requirement of the Proposal sent stage is recognised but not yet checked: it is switched on with the quote builder (3b). Reversible migration (one type and seven columns on deals; existing deals get their stage-entry date from their history). Tests: 9 unit and 10 integration.

## Not yet merged (branch `feature/crm-phase-2b`, on top of #96, no migration)

- **Sales module, phase 2b: import, export, and account and contact pages.** **Import** leads, accounts or contacts from a CSV (Sales > Import and Export): download the template, pick the file, and every row is checked before anything is saved (phones turned into +234 form, emails lower-cased, choices matched by name in any case, the same company, phone or email twice in the file warned, up to 1,000 rows). Rows with errors are never imported; a download gives back just the failed rows with what is wrong, ready to correct and upload again. Rows that look like something already held are flagged (a match owned by someone else is described only by its owner) and left out unless you choose to import them anyway. A sales person owns what they import; a manager's leads follow the owner method. **Marketing consent is recorded only when the file says Yes or No and says where it came from**, as a new permanent record. Contacts are attached to an account by its exact name (the account must exist and be one you can see). **Export** gives the records you may see, with the same columns the import reads plus owner and status; **cells a spreadsheet could run as a formula are made harmless**, and every export is audited as a count. **Account and contact pages** show the details with related contacts and deals, and the contact page shows the full consent history (including that of records merged into it) with a form to record a new consent or withdrawal. The file itself is never kept. Tests: 12 unit and 8 integration.

## Merged: failed-login audit (#79, branch `failed-login-audit`)

- **The two-factor step can no longer be guessed without limit** (a change to sign-in, approved by the founder). Before, any number of wrong codes was accepted (nothing counted, locked or logged them), and a correct password reset the failure count, so wrong guesses could be mixed with right passwords and never reach the lock. Now **a wrong code counts on the same counter as a wrong password: five in total lock the account for 15 minutes**, and a locked account refuses even the right code. For an account with two-factor on, **a correct password no longer clears the count; only the whole sign-in succeeding does** (an account without two-factor is unchanged). The message for a wrong code is still "Incorrect code.". The count is raised in one database step, so many guesses sent at the same moment cannot read the same old number.
- **Failed sign-ins and related failures are now in the audit log** (rule 5), in the account's own organization, with the address and the browser, and never the password, code, reset token or sign-in challenge. New actions: `LOGIN_FAILED` (reasons `WRONG_PASSWORD`, `ACCOUNT_LOCKED`, `ACCOUNT_INACTIVE`), `TOTP_FAILED`, `PASSWORD_RESET_REQUESTED`, `PASSWORD_RESET_FAILED` (`TOKEN_USED`, `TOKEN_EXPIRED`), `PASSWORD_CHANGE_FAILED`, `TOTP_DISABLE_FAILED`; `ACCOUNT_LOCKED` now says whether passwords or codes caused it. Attempts on a locked or switched-off account are written at most once every five minutes. The audit report has a **Show: Security events** filter. An email with **no account** goes to the application log only, as a keyed hash plus the address (never the email, because people type passwords in the email box). The replies to the person signing in are unchanged. **Not done:** a limit by IP address (waits for the host decision); a limit on wrong current passwords or codes on the change-password and turn-off-two-factor screens. No pay figure changes; no migration; no new dependency.

## Previously not yet merged (branch `verified-settings-gate`, now #77)

- **Payroll is blocked while a required statutory rule is unverified** (rule 3). For a **production client** (a client of the bureau whose engagement is **ACTIVE**), the PAYE, pension and employer-contribution rules in force for the period must each be exactly what **HR and Finance both signed** in the bureau's Statutory Rule Library, or the payroll is stopped. The check is made when a payroll is calculated, when a supplementary payroll is created, and when a payroll is **submitted, approved and locked**, so a rule changed after the calculation cannot slip through. It checks the rule's content against what was signed, so a rate changed directly in the database after sign-off is caught; how the rule reached the client (published, or copied when it was onboarded) does not matter. A rule the bureau is paying on that was **never put through the library** (a "baseline") counts as unverified, which matches the launch checklist. **There is no override**: the message names the rule and says to verify it in the library. **Every block is written to the audit log** (who tried, which step, which rule, why). Clients still being onboarded, the bureau's own organization and organizations that are not bureau clients are not stopped, but the payroll page now tells a person plainly which rules are not yet verified, and, when all are, who verified them and when. **No pay figure changes** (all 401 seeded figures are identical). The demo client now goes through the real propose, verify and publish steps and goes live, so it is gated and passes for the right reason. **Before the first real client goes ACTIVE, the bureau must run its current PAYE, pension and employer-contribution rules through the library once.** No migration.

## Known gaps

See `docs/rules-conformity.md` for the full list against the project rules, and `docs/threeroots-gap-analysis.md` for the product gaps. The largest: money is calculated in floating-point numbers, statutory rules have no "verified" gate on a payroll run, there are no golden tests or feature flags, field encryption is unbuilt, and migrations can't be reversed.
