# CRM Phase 9a plan: privacy (the Nigeria Data Protection Act features)

**Status: built on branch `feature/crm-phase-9a` after the founder's "continue" (2026-10-30). Phase 9 is split, as phases 1, 3, 5 and 7 were: 9a is privacy; 9b is the role matrix, rate limits, pagination, indexes and the formal security review (Appendix B), and follows.** The choices below are mine, listed for you to confirm. The wording of consent, the response time and how long data may be kept are for a lawyer to review.

## What the pack asks for in Phase 9, and what 9a builds
1. **One-click unsubscribe in every marketing message, and a landing page that records the withdrawal immediately.** Every marketing **email** now carries an unsubscribe link in its text and as the standard `List-Unsubscribe` header (with one-click support), and **a marketing WhatsApp must contain the link** (the server refuses to log one that does not). The link is a signed token (no login, nothing to guess); opening it records the withdrawal at once. A bad or altered link does nothing and says nothing about who it was for.
2. **Suppression enforced centrally.** Withdrawing marketing consent (by the link, or by a person recording it) also **suppresses the person's email address and phone numbers**, so the same address on another contact or lead, or on a re-imported record, is blocked too, even if that record says "granted". The suppression list holds **only a keyed hash of the address**, never the address, so it still works after the person has been erased. The check is in the one function every marketing send goes through (email, WhatsApp, and the automation rules). Recording a fresh consent for a contact lifts the suppression of that contact's own addresses (they chose to come back); an erased person's suppression is never lifted.
3. **Data subject requests for a contact or a lead:** a register (number, who asked, when, due date, identity checked, who handled it, completion date), an **export of everything held about the person**, and **erasure**. Erasure **anonymises** the person: it replaces their name, job title, phone numbers and email, blanks their notes, consent evidence, and the text of the activities, tasks and cases linked to them, and clears the site-contact details on handovers that match their phone or email. **Audit entries are kept** (they hold ids, never personal values). Anything that could still mention them in free text elsewhere (a deal name, someone else's note) cannot be found with certainty, so the tool **lists the records to review** instead of claiming they are clean.
4. **Retention:** an administrator sets how many months an unconverted lead may sit with no activity (off until set). Leads past it are listed for review: **keep** (for a further period) or **erase** (through the same erasure, recorded as a retention request).
5. **Masking:** contact and lead exports show phone numbers and emails **masked** (for example `+234*******567`, `f***@lekki.test`) to roles that can read sales records but not change them (Finance, Operations); sales people, managers and the company administrator get the full values.

## How the immutable records are erased
Consent rows, activities, case history and handover history are protected by database triggers that refuse any change. **Erasure is the one exception, and only inside the erasure transaction, only for the personal columns** (consent source and evidence; an activity's subject, body and survey; a case note; a handover note). The transaction sets a database flag that lasts only until it ends; nothing else can set it from the app, and every other change to those rows is still refused (tested).

## Choices I made (please confirm or correct)
1. **The response time is 30 days** from receipt (NDPA: one month); it is shown as the due date and can be changed later.
2. **Only the company administrator** (a new permission, `sales.privacy.manage`) may open, answer or erase a request.
3. **A record is anonymised, not deleted** (so deals, cases and the audit trail keep their links): the contact becomes "Erased Contact", voided, with an erased date.
4. **The unsubscribe link needs `APP_URL`** (the public address of the app). Without it in production a marketing message is refused rather than sent without a link. **It also depends on `AUTH_SECRET`: changing that secret breaks old unsubscribe links and the suppression hashes**, so it must not be rotated casually.
5. **Opening the link records the withdrawal straight away** (a link scanner opening it by mistake only ever withdraws a marketing consent: the safe direction); there is a plain message saying how to come back.
6. **Free text elsewhere is reviewed by a person**, not rewritten by the tool (see 3 above).

## What it adds
Tables `CrmSuppression` (keyed hashes), `CrmDataRequest`; columns `leadRetentionMonths` (setting), `erasedAt` (contact, lead) and `retentionKeepUntil` (lead); a public page `/unsubscribe/[token]`; a Privacy page for the administrator. Migration `20261111090000_sales_privacy`, reversible. No payroll, statutory or contract table is touched.

## Tests first
Token signing and tampering; masking; the unsubscribe flow and its idempotency; suppression across email, WhatsApp and automation, and across two records with the same address; every marketing email and WhatsApp carrying the link, service ones not needing it; the data request register and its deadline; export completeness for a contact with consent history, activities, tasks, cases, a converted lead, a deal and a handover site contact; erasure leaving no personal data in the CRM tables (a scan of every text column for the person's name, email and phone), keeping the audit entries, keeping the person suppressed, and refusing without a verified identity or without the permission; the triggers still refusing every other change; retention listing, keeping and erasing; masking by role; organization isolation throughout.
