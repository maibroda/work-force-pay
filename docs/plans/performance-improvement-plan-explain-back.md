# Explain-back note: the performance improvement plan (PIP)

Branch `performance-improvement-plans`. One migration, `20261129090000_performance_improvement_plans` (additive: four new tables, three enums, five settings columns). This adds **a process that is run against a named employee, and two approval steps**, so it touches access control, approvals and the audit log: please read it. Built on your "go" of 2026-11-07 to the plan `docs/plans/performance-improvement-plan.md`. **You did not answer the plan's seven questions, so I used my recommended answers; they are listed in "Assumptions" below and each is easy to change.**

## The rules, step by step

1. **HR drafts.** Someone with `pip.manage` (HR administrator and above) names an **active** employee (or one on leave), the concerns (at least a sentence), a start date, a length (default 60 days, longest 90 days) and a **plan owner**: an active person in this company whose role can run plans. The **end date is the start date plus the length**. **An employee can have only one open plan** (Draft, Awaiting approval, Active or Outcome awaiting approval): the employee's record is locked while this is checked, so two drafts at once cannot both pass.
2. **Objectives.** Each has a gap, a measurable target (in words), the support the company will give, and a due date **inside the plan's dates**. They can be added or removed only while the plan is a draft. A plan with no objective cannot be submitted.
3. **A second person starts it.** Submitting moves the plan to "Awaiting approval to start". Someone with `pip.approve` (company administrator, super administrator) **who is neither the drafter nor the plan owner** starts it; the server refuses otherwise, even if the person holds the right. The approver can instead **return it to draft with a reason**. Only when it starts does the employee see it, and the first review date is set (start date plus the review interval, 14 days).
4. **Reviews.** Only on an Active plan, dated **not in the future and not before the start**, with a summary and a result (Met, Partly met, Not met) for each objective assessed. A review is **never edited afterwards**; an objective's result is its **latest** assessment. The next review falls due one interval after the review, never after the end date.
5. **The outcome.** The owner or HR proposes Successful, Extended or Unsuccessful, with a note, **only after at least one review is on record**. An extension must name the new end date: **later than the current one, by at most 30 days, and only once** (the company's settings). **A second person approves**: someone with `pip.approve` who is **neither the person who proposed it nor the owner**. Successful and Unsuccessful **close** the plan for good; Extended keeps it Active with the new end date. The approver can **return** an outcome with a reason, and the plan is Active again.
6. **Nothing else happens.** An Unsuccessful outcome **changes nothing about the employee** (no status, no pay, no sanction, no exit): it is only recorded and shown on the plan and in the HR reminders. HR decides what follows through the existing processes.
7. **The employee** sees the plan from the moment it starts (not a draft, not a plan waiting to start, not an outcome that is not yet approved). They **acknowledge once**: Agree, or Disagree with a reason; a disagreement does not stop the plan and HR sees it. They may add **one comment to each review**. Nobody can answer for them, and another employee cannot reach their plan (the server says "not found").
8. **Withdrawing.** A draft or a plan waiting to start can be withdrawn, with a reason, by whoever manages plans; a plan that has started needs the approve right. A closed or withdrawn plan can never be reopened; a new plan is a new record.
9. **Audit.** Every step is logged by **ids and field names only**: never what a concern, objective, note, comment or response says (so a person can be erased without touching the append-only log). The HR reminders show names and dates, never the concerns.

## A worked example

A guard's appraisal ends with the recommendation "Performance improvement plan" and the note "arrives late to post". HR opens the appraisal and clicks **Draft a plan from this appraisal**: start 1 November, default 60 days, so the end date is **31 December**; owner = the HR administrator. HR adds one objective ("no late arrival in the last 30 days", support "shift swap to a closer post", due 10 December) and submits. The HR administrator **cannot start it** (no approve right); **the company administrator can**, and does on 1 November: the plan is Active and the first review falls due on **15 November** (1 November plus 14 days). On 14 November the owner records a review: objective Partly met. The next review is due **28 November**. The guard has seen the plan since 1 November and recorded "Disagree: the bus on my route was delayed", which the owner can read. On 28 November the objective is Met; the owner proposes **Successful**. The owner cannot approve it; the company administrator can. The plan closes as Successful; nothing changes in the guard's pay or status.

## What could go wrong, and what proves it does not

| Risk | Test |
| --- | --- |
| A plan is started or its outcome approved by the person who drafted, owns or proposed it | `pip.test.ts` (unit, `approverConflict`) and the integration tests: the drafter, the owner and the proposer are each refused, even holding `pip.approve` |
| A step is skipped (approving a draft, approving an outcome nobody proposed, reopening a closed plan) | the status-move tests: every jump that skips a step is refused; closed and withdrawn have no way out |
| An extension beyond the limits, or a plan longer than allowed | the length and extension tests, including the company changing its own limits and the default never exceeding the longest |
| The employee sees a draft, an unapproved outcome, or another employee's plan | the employee-view tests: nothing before it starts; no outcome until approved; another employee's plan and review are "not found" |
| A role that should not see plans does (finance, operations, supervisor, payroll, the employee through the HR screens) | the role tests: each is refused on every read and action; the auditor can read but not change |
| Another company's plans, owners or employees leak | the cross-company tests: not found, empty lists, an owner from another company refused |
| The audit log or the reminders carry what a concern or comment says | the audit test searches every `PIP_*` entry for each secret phrase; the reminders carry names and dates only |
| A review is edited or backdated | reviews are never edited by any function; dates in the future or before the start are refused |
| An appraisal starts a plan on its own | the appraisal tests: a plan is never created by approving an appraisal; only an explicit draft, only from an approved appraisal that recommended one |
| The migration loses data | round trip (up, down, up) on a copy of the seeded database: employees, appraisals and the HR policy unchanged and the schema identical |

## Assumptions (my recommended answers to the plan's questions; change any of them)

1. **A PIP is its own workflow**, separate from the Disciplinary area. 2. **HR runs the plans**, with a named plan owner chosen from people who can run plans; **no line-manager access** in this step (the reporting-manager link exists, and a manager view can be added if you want it). 3. **Limits:** default 60 days, longest 90, one extension of at most 30 days, a review every 14 days: **proposed values, to be confirmed and checked by a lawyer.** 4. **Second-person approval to start and to approve the outcome.** 5. **Nothing automatic after Unsuccessful**; no "capability" case type yet. 6. **The employee sees, acknowledges and comments**; no evidence upload yet. 7. **Legal review of the wording is yours to arrange**: the screens say what the system does, not what the law requires.

Smaller choices I made that you did not ask for: a **target is free text** (the plan also mentioned an optional number and unit; I left it out so no number is ever compared or rounded); a **review result** is Met, Partly met or Not met; a **withdrawal** of a plan that has started needs the approve right but **not** a second person (it ends the process in the employee's favour); **an employee on leave can be given a plan**, one who has left cannot (the HR reminders list open plans of leavers so HR can withdraw them); and a plan's **owner may also be the drafter** (the approver is the second person).
