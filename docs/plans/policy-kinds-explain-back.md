# Handbook and procedures: explain-back note (slice 4B)

Written 2026-11-17 for the founder's approval of 2026-11-17 (section 6 of `hr-phases-4-5-plan.md` as recommended, and the choice of 2026-11-17 to start slice 4B). **No money, pay or statutory setting is touched.** It extends the **policy area** (versions, effective dates, audience, acknowledgements) instead of building a second copy. One additive migration. **A policy created the old way behaves exactly as before.**

## What the code does, step by step

1. **A document has a kind: policy, handbook or procedure (SOP).** It is chosen when the document is made and **cannot be changed afterwards**. Every existing policy is a **policy** (the migration's default).
2. **Acknowledgement is a switch.** For a **policy it is on** (as always). For a **handbook or a procedure it is off by default**. HR can **switch it either way for any item**, at creation or later. When it is off, employees **read** the document and **are never asked to sign it**: it is **not** in "still to acknowledge", **not** chased, **not** in the HR digest's overdue acknowledgements, **not** in the acknowledgement report, and **an acknowledgement of it is refused** (by the employee, or recorded on their behalf).
3. **Switching it on or off.** Switching **on** asks people from then on, using the same grace period; **what people already acknowledged for the current version stands** (switch it off and on again and they are still acknowledged). Switching **off** stops the chase at once. Every change is audited.
4. **Versions, effective dates and the audience by category work exactly as for policies.** A new version of a document that needs no acknowledgement **replaces the earlier wording and asks nobody to sign**. A document is shown to an employee only once it has taken effect and only if it is for their category.
5. **An optional review date.** HR may give any document a **review-due day**. **Once that day has passed**, the item appears in the **HR digest** ("Policies, handbook and procedures past their review date", with a link) **until it is marked reviewed**. Marking it reviewed (HR with the configure right) records the review, sets the **next review day (not in the past) or none**, and is audited by ids and dates. A document archived, or with no date, never appears.
6. **What employees see.** On **My policies**: what they must acknowledge (exactly as before), **a new "Handbook & procedures" section** (read-only, each item opens to its text, with a **search by title or summary**, as plain text, case-insensitive), and what they have acknowledged (as before).
7. **What HR sees.** The list shows each document's kind, its review date (red when overdue) and "not required" instead of an acknowledgement count; the item's page shows the kind, whether acknowledgement is required and the review date, hides the acknowledgement table for an item that needs none, and gains a "Mark as reviewed" form and the two new settings.

## Where each rule comes from

The founder's approval of section 6 of the plan, as recommended: **K1** kinds (policy, handbook, SOP), **K2** acknowledgement required for a policy and not for a handbook or SOP, switchable per item, **K3** an optional review date with a reminder when overdue, **K4** written by those who write policies today (the right to configure policies).

## Worked example

HR publishes **"Cash handling"** as a **procedure** (SOP) for everyone, effective today, with a review due on 10 November. **No acknowledgement is required** (the default for a procedure). All 110 employees can **read** it under My policies; **nobody is asked to sign it**; the HR list shows "not required" instead of "x/110". Once 10 November has passed, the **HR digest lists** "Cash handling (Procedure (SOP)) — review was due 2026-11-10" with a link. HR reviews it and **marks it reviewed with the next review on 17 November 2027**: it drops out of the digest. Later HR decides the procedure must be signed and **switches acknowledgement on**: from that day the 110 employees are asked (with the normal 14 days' grace), and the digest and the acknowledgement report include it. **Switching it off again** stops the chase and keeps the acknowledgements already given.

## What could go wrong, and what I did not do

- **Switching acknowledgement on for an old, widely read document makes many people "overdue" at once** if its effective date is long past (the grace period counts from the later of the effective day and joining). That is how policies already behave; HR should expect it.
- **A document with no acknowledgement has no record that anyone read it.** That is the choice (K2). Switch acknowledgement on where a record is needed.
- **No file upload:** a document is text or a reference, as policies are today, until a host and the encryption key exist (slice D).
- **The review is a date, not a workflow:** nothing reminds the author by email except the HR digest; no second person approves a review.
- **Not built:** a mandatory review date for procedures (you approved "optional"; say if you want it mandatory every 12 months), searching inside the text (title and summary only), and a separate "knowledge base".
- **Not browser-checked:** the pages as finance, the auditor or a manager (the tests and CI's smoke test cover the roles).
