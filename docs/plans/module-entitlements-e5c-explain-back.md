# E5c explain-back note: applying an accepted blueprint to a client (built; nothing is enforced yet)

Written 2026-11-04. For the founder. This changes what a client company can reach (its plan and modules), so it touches access control and is a critical change under `CLAUDE.md`. **Nothing is enforced anywhere yet (every module's switch is OFF), so applying a blueprint cannot lock a client out of anything today; it changes what the client is recorded as having.**

1. **What it does.** When a blueprint is accepted, and its lead has become one of the bureau's clients, an authorised person can "Apply to the client". The blueprint's proposed plan, add-ons and limits (employees, sales users) become that client's subscription: created if the client has none, changed if it has one (which is the usual case, because onboarding gives a new client a default plan). The subscription's history records that it came from this blueprint, and the blueprint records when and to which client it was applied.
2. **All or nothing.** The plan change, the limits, the history entry and the "applied" mark are one database transaction. If any step fails, including a second person applying the same blueprint at the same moment, nothing is changed.
3. **Once only.** A blueprint can be applied once. It stays "accepted" (applying is recorded, not a new status).
4. **Who may.** Platform staff and a bureau's administrator for the bureau's own client, also while working inside that client. **Not the sales person who made the blueprint**: changing a client's modules is subscription work, and sales people can quote but not change what a client has. Another bureau cannot even see the blueprint; an ordinary company's administrator is refused.
5. **It never strands work.** The E5a blockers apply to every module the change would end: a pay run in progress, a journal awaiting posting, a period being closed and so on refuse the application and change nothing (the blueprint stays unapplied); a module whose work would only be left readable needs an acknowledgement, given by the second button.
6. **Which client.** Only the client the blueprint's lead became (`clientOrganizationId` on the lead), and it must be a client of the same bureau that made the blueprint. No lead, a lead that has not become a client, or another bureau's client are each refused with a plain message.
7. **What could go wrong.** (a) A wrong blueprint applied gives a client the wrong modules: it is undone with a normal change of plan by platform staff, and the history shows both. (b) If a bureau's client was onboarded with a larger plan than the blueprint, applying ends the extra modules (subject to the blockers): that is what "the blueprint is what was sold" means, and the blueprint page says what it proposes. (c) The limits are recorded and not yet checked anywhere (E6).
8. **Worked example.** Acme Facilities was onboarded with the default Payroll Core plan. Its blueprint (accepted) proposes HR + Payroll with Analytics added, for 80 employees. The bureau's administrator presses "Apply to the client": Acme now has Core, HR, Payroll, Self-service and Analytics, an employee limit of 80, a history entry "Plan changed, applied from an accepted blueprint", and the blueprint shows "Applied on 4 Nov". Pressing it again is refused. If Acme had a period still being closed in Accounting and the blueprint did not include Accounting, the application would be refused with "Accounting: 1 accounting period is still being closed. Close or reopen it first.", and nothing would change.
9. **Which tests prove it.** `tests/integration/entitlements-provision.test.ts`: the plan, add-ons and limits applied with the blueprint in the history; creating when none exists; refusal for a draft, a dropped or an already applied blueprint; the lead and bureau rules; who may and may not; working from inside the client; a blocker refusing and changing nothing; and the audit entries in both organizations.
10. **Honest limits.** I wrote the tests first but ran them only after the code existed. The page buttons have not been looked at in a browser. Dated changes ("from the end of the month") and the read-only window are not built.
