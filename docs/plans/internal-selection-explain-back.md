# Internal applicants: what "selected" means (recruitment slice B, part 2): explain-back note

Written 2026-11-17 for the founder's approval of 2026-11-17 of `internal-selection-plan.md` (S1 to S3, S5 and S6 as recommended; S4 left with HR as today; S7 as a separate fix). **No pay, contract or salary structure is set or changed.** It reuses the **existing promotion process** (career) instead of building a second one. One additive, reversible migration.

## What the code does, step by step

1. **Selecting an internal applicant starts a promotion proposal and nothing else.** On an internal applicant's candidate page, once they are at the **offer stage**, HR sees **"Selected: start the move"**. HR gives an **intended date** (not in the past) and writes **the reason in their own words**. The system opens a **promotion proposal for that employee to the vacancy's role**, linked to the candidate. **Nothing from the interviews (feedback, scores, recommendation) is copied.**
2. **Who:** the **HR administrator only** (it needs the right to assess candidates **and** the right to propose promotions). Not the administrators, finance, payroll, the auditor, a manager, the applicant or anyone else.
3. **A candidate a panel interviewed** needs the chair's recommendation and a second person's approval first, **exactly as an offer would** (the move cannot start before that).
4. **The existing promotion rules then apply unchanged:** a **different HR person approves** (never the one who started it); if the system warned (no career path, too little time in role, a missing course, an open flag) **a written reason is needed to approve**; **the employee sees it only once it is approved, and their manager sees nothing**; **nobody is told by the system**.
5. **A move to the role they already hold** (a lateral move) is **refused** with a message pointing to the employee screens (S2): the promotion process only changes a role.
6. **No offer for an internal applicant (S3).** The offer form is gone for them and an offer cannot be raised; the approved promotion takes its place. So the **old exception** (an approver sees an internal applicant once an offer exists) **is removed**: an internal applicant is read by HR and the panel only, whatever stage they reach.
7. **Positions on the vacancy.** A vacancy has a number of positions. A **live move** (proposed, approved or applied) **holds its position against an outside hire**; a **declined or withdrawn** proposal **gives it back**. The position is **filled** only when the promotion is **applied**.
8. **Applying.** HR changes the role (and the pay, through the existing screens) and marks the promotion applied, as today. **If it was started from an internal applicant, the candidate becomes "Moved"** (a new stage, never "Hired") **and the vacancy is marked filled once every position is filled.** A candidate with a live proposal **cannot be rejected or withdrawn** until the proposal is withdrawn, so the two records never disagree.
9. **Counting (S6).** An internal move **is not a hire**: the hiring figures stay about new people. The requisition's page says, for example, **"1 of 1 position(s) filled (1 by internal move)"**.
10. **Erasure (a gap found on the way, fixed here).** Erasing a former employee **now also clears their application as an internal applicant** (name, note, interview words, and the applicant's own name on the record): the old code looked only for the candidate record a person was *hired from*, so an internal applicant's name and note would have survived.

## Where each rule comes from

The founder's approval of `internal-selection-plan.md` (2026-11-17): **S1** a promotion proposal through the existing process, **S2** no lateral moves in this slice, **S3** no offer for an internal applicant and the approver exception removed, **S5** HR administrator only, **S6** a move fills a position but is not a hire. **S4** (how a promotion in the middle of a payroll month is paid) **was not decided and is not built**: pay stays with HR on the existing screens.

## Worked example

Chioma, an **office clerk**, applies inside the company for the **Contract Manager** vacancy (one position). After the interviews HR moves her to the offer stage. HR clicks **Start the move**, picks **1 December** and writes "Strong site record and knows the contracts". A **promotion proposal** appears (office clerk to Contract Manager) with the usual warning "no career path". The **Company Administrator** approves it, writing why the missing career path does not matter. Chioma is told **by HR** (the system told nobody, including her manager). HR changes her role and pay on the existing screens and marks the promotion applied: **she is shown as "Moved"**, the vacancy shows **"1 of 1 position(s) filled (1 by internal move)"** and is marked **filled**, and the hiring figures are unchanged.

## What could go wrong, and what I did not do

- **Pay can lag the role** (the role is changed but the pay is changed late). Nothing here makes that worse or fixes it; the existing digest flags an approved promotion not applied after 14 days.
- **The applicant's manager learns only from HR.** The system tells nobody, so HR must tell the manager at the right moment; nothing prompts HR to.
- **A promotion applied some other way** (the role changed on the employee screen and a different proposal applied) does not touch a vacancy: only a proposal **started from the candidate page** is linked to one.
- **A lateral move** (same role, another department, site or manager) **has no process**: HR records it on the employee screens and closes the application with a reason (S2).
- **The reason is free text about a person.** It is cleared by the existing erasure (promotion reasons already were). The audit entry carries ids only.
- **Not built:** pay, a lateral-move process, a message to anyone, the requisition's justification in the audit log (S7: a separate small fix), and a candidate's own data copy including their internal applications (a question for the lawyer, as before).
- **Not browser-checked** as finance, the auditor or a manager (the tests and CI's smoke test cover the roles; no new page was added).
