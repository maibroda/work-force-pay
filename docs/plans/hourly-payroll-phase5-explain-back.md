# Explain-back note: hourly pay, phase 5 (the hourly reports)

Phase 5 of `hourly-payroll-assessment.md`, started on the founder's "go with phase 5" of 2026-11-19. **No migration and no new table**: it only reads what phases 2 and 3 already keep. Files: `src/lib/payroll/hourly-report.ts` (the arithmetic), `src/server/services/hourly-reports.ts` (the four reports), and small wiring in `src/server/report-registry.ts`, `src/lib/report-access.ts` and the Reports menu in `src/lib/nav.ts`. Tests: `tests/unit/hourly-report.test.ts` (12) and `tests/integration/hourly-reports.test.ts` (12). The reports use the existing Reports screens and CSV download.

**The cost shown is INDICATIVE, not payroll.** The pay run does not read hours yet (phase 4), so no payslip or payment changes. The cost is the approved hours multiplied by the approved hourly rates, worked the way the pay run will find them. Payroll, when it reads hours, may group its lines differently and so differ by a kobo.

**What it does, step by step**
1. **Only approved hours count.** A draft, a submitted shift, a rejected or withdrawn one, or one replaced by a correction never counts towards hours or cost. A corrected shift counts only as its correction, once that is approved.
2. **Each approved piece is priced** with the rate in force on the day it was worked, in your order (the employee's own rate, then the contract and job role, then the job role). A rate that changes mid-month prices each day at its own rate.
3. **One grain, one rounding.** A line is one person, one contract, one month, one class of day and one rate. Its amount is the minutes x the rate / 60, rounded half up to the kobo once. All four reports are roll-ups of the same lines, so by-employee, by-client and month-by-month add up to the same total and nothing is counted twice.
4. **Hours with no approved rate are never priced as zero.** They are shown apart ("Hours with no approved rate") and left out of the cost, so a gap in the rates is always visible.
5. **The four reports.** *By employee*: weekday, weekend and public holiday hours and cost for each person. *By client and contract*: the same for each client and contract, with the number of people. *Month by month*: each month, with the change in hours and cost on the month before; a shift over a month end is split between the two months. *Approved and waiting*: for each person, the approved hours (the ones that will be paid) against the hours waiting for approval, drafts, shifts with no clock-out (counted, not given hours) and rejected or withdrawn ones. All take a date range; the first three also a client; by employee and status also a person.
6. **Who may open them.** The three cost reports show pay, so they need the payroll right. The approved-and-waiting report shows hours only, no money, and needs only the reports right. With hourly pay switched off every one is empty and says why. Everything is for the caller's company only.

**Where each rule comes from.** Rates and their order: H1 (your answer of 2026-11-19). Approved hours only, breaks deducted, holiday by the work location's state: H9, H5, H11 (phase 3). Rounding half up once per line: the project's money rule (rule 2). Hours shown to two decimals are for display only; every total is kept in minutes and kobo.

**Worked example** (invented rates: role weekday 1,000, weekend 1,500, holiday 2,000 an hour). A guard works Friday 19:00 to Saturday 07:00 with a break 02:00-02:30, and the Saturday is a Lagos-only holiday. The shift is approved. The by-employee report shows 5 weekday hours (5,000) and 6.5 holiday hours (13,000): 11.5 hours, 18,000. A second person with their own rate of 1,800 for weekends works 8 hours on a Sunday: 14,400. A third works a Friday-to-Saturday shift over 31 July: the month-by-month report shows 5 hours (5,000) in July and 7 hours (10,500) in August. A fourth person has no hourly rate for their role: their 8 approved hours appear under "no approved rate" and add nothing to the cost. A shift that is only submitted shows in the approved-and-waiting report as 8 hours waiting, and nowhere in the cost.

**What could go wrong, and what is NOT covered**
- **Indicative, not payroll** (above). Do not use these figures as what a person will be paid until phase 4 is built.
- **A person's job role is taken as it is today.** If a person changed role, an earlier month is priced with the current role's rate unless they have their own rate. (Time entries do not record the role.)
- **Budget against actual is NOT built**, because there is no budget in the application to compare with. Where a budget comes from (a figure per contract, per month, per client?) is for you to decide.
- **Hours by the day worked, and the status report by the day the shift started.** A shift over midnight at the edge of the dates asked for can be counted in one report's range and not in the other's.
- **Not covered:** hours shown in the report are rounded to two decimals for display (the totals are exact from minutes); no page for supervisors only for their own sites (the reports right is company-wide, as for the work register).
