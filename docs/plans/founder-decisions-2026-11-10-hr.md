## B. What changes, and what is done about it

1. **Skills level names and definitions (in the unmerged skills build): changed now.** Level 1 Basic (understands the skill but needs supervision), 2 Developing (routine tasks with limited supervision), 3 Proficient (independently performs to the required standard), 4 Advanced (expertise; can coach or guide others). The plan used Aware, Basic, Proficient, Expert.
2. **Probation workflow: needs a plan.** The founder's flow is: the reporting manager **recommends** (confirm, extend or not confirm); **HR reviews** against the probation policy; an **authorised independent manager or department head makes the final decision**; the employee receives **documented feedback and a chance to respond**; a failed probation triggers no automatic exit. Built today: HR decides, with the appraisal's reviewer and signer excluded, no recommendation step, no independent final decider and no employee response. This changes a critical module, so it needs its own plan.
3. **Surveys: the plan is revised** (`docs/plans/engagement-surveys-plan-revision.md`). (a) **A group under ten may take part**; only its group-level display is suppressed (combined into a larger group, or hidden). (b) **Hybrid participation from the first release**: the portal, a QR code, a shared company device, and an assisted session by an authorised HR representative (never a supervisor), with a **unique access code per employee**. (c) 24 months for raw answers, then deletion, with anonymised aggregates kept. **One design choice needs your confirmation:** the register says responses are "confidential" and "identifiable raw responses" are deleted; the plan stores answers with **no link to the person at all**, which is stricter (and still deletes raw answers at 24 months).
4. **No HR administrator, and an outsourced HR administrator: needs a plan.** A **Delegated HR Officer** role, appointed by the Company Administrator, receiving only explicitly assigned permissions; welfare cases assignable to them; **succession stays disabled until an authorised HR role exists**; sensitive welfare records never become visible to the Company Administrator; a notice that some functions need an authorised officer; optional **outsourced HR administrator** (an external consultant). Today `welfare.manage` and `succession.manage` are held by the HR administrator only. See `docs/plans/hr-governance-plan.md`.
5. **Retention as a configurable policy: needs a plan.** Default periods per record category, **expiry notifications, deletion or anonymisation, legal holds that suspend deletion, no bypass without authorisation, and auditable deletion**. Today the system has an employee retention setting and erasure of free text, not a per-category schedule. See `docs/plans/hr-governance-plan.md`.
6. **Role separation on sensitive transactions: needs a plan.** "No one initiates, approves and independently audits the same sensitive transaction": probation decisions, improvement plans, disciplinary actions, **welfare case closures** and **succession nominations**. Built today: improvement plans and probation have maker and checker rules; **welfare closure and succession nominations have none**. See `docs/plans/hr-governance-plan.md`.
7. **Succession read-logging:** the register wants **access** logged, not only changes. Not built; part of the governance plan.
8. **Training evaluation, richer: needs a small plan.** Three stages: (1) the **participant** evaluation covers relevance, content, facilitator and delivery (built: one overall rating and a comment), (2) the **manager** evaluation of application (built), (3) an **effectiveness evaluation** of whether the training improved job performance, measured separately from attendance (not built). The register also lets HR read individual responses "only where authorised and necessary"; **the build is stricter** (never by name): please confirm you want to keep it that way.
9. **Employee data export:** confidential management information (succession, other employees' information, **confidential investigation records**) must be excluded. **Checked: already so.** Succession is never included; an employee-relations case marked confidential (harassment, whistleblowing, and every welfare case) is left out of the copy and only **counted** (the copy says how many were withheld), and non-confidential cases and disciplinary records are included as before.
10. **Feedback:** "negative feedback must rest on specific observations, not opinions" is guidance for the screen wording; I will add it to the feedback form text. The auditor reading feedback *metadata* (who, when, acknowledged) is already available through the audit log.

## C. The retention defaults proposed (configurable; a lawyer validates)

Six years after exit for employee, appraisal, probation, feedback, improvement-plan, disciplinary, training and skills records; six years after closure for welfare cases; succession reviewed annually; raw survey responses 24 months; anonymised survey reports indefinitely; audit logs six years; unsuccessful applicants two years after the recruitment closes; access and authentication logs two years after account closure. **The founder's own note says six years must not be applied to every record without legal advice** (the Labour Act's three-year record rule, the data protection "no longer than necessary" principle and the separate tax and accounting rules differ): the schedule is therefore configurable, with these as defaults only.
