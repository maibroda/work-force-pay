# Recruitment slice A: interview panels, scorecards and the comparison page (explain-back note)

Written 2026-11-17 for the founder's approval of 2026-11-16 ("approve section 5 as recommended and go for slice A"), from `recruitment-depth-1a-plan.md`. **No money, pay, statutory setting or bank file is touched.** It touches **access control** (a new right and a new kind of reader of candidate data), **the step in front of an offer**, and **one migration** (additive, reversible). Please read the "Where I departed from, or chose within, the plan" list.

## What the code does, step by step

1. **Criteria.** HR (the new right `recruitment.assess`, held by the HR administrator alone) sets three to six named criteria for a requisition. They can be changed until the first scorecard is submitted.
2. **Panel.** HR names a panel for an interview: **two to five employees, one of them the chair**, each still employed and **with a login**, nobody twice. Not once a scorecard is in, and not for an interview already scored the old way. **An interview with a panel can no longer take the old single feedback.**
3. **Scoring.** A panel member opens **My interviews** and sees **only the interviews they sit on**. They score **every criterion from 1 to 5** and give a recommendation (strong hire, hire, no hire, strong no hire), with optional comments. **They cannot read anyone else's scorecard until the scoring is closed.** They can correct their own until then.
4. **Closing.** It closes **by itself when the last member has submitted**, or **the chair or HR closes it earlier** (at least one scorecard is needed). Closing **completes the interview**, so the candidate can be moved to the offer stage exactly as after an old-style interview. A closed panel's scorecards cannot change.
5. **Comparison.** For a requisition, HR sees each candidate **in the order of their candidate numbers, never by score**. While a panel is open HR sees **only how many have submitted**; once closed, every scorecard and, per criterion, the **lowest, highest, average (one decimal, half up) and the number who scored**, with the recommendations counted. A panel member sees only the **closed** interviews they sat on. The page says in plain words that it is advice and never orders, filters or rejects anyone.
6. **Selection.** The **chair of a closed panel** recommends selecting the candidate, with a reason of at least ten characters. **Someone else who holds the approval right** (`hr.approve`: the HR administrator or the Company Administrator, **never the chair**) approves or declines (a decline needs a reason). **An offer for a candidate a panel interviewed cannot be raised without the approved selection.** A candidate interviewed the old way is unaffected.
7. **Who reads interview feedback.** The score, recommendation and words of the **old single feedback** and of the new scorecards are read by **the HR administrator (recruitment.assess) and the panel members** only. **The administrators, finance and the auditor still see that interviews took place** (round and date) **but not what was said.** Every read by HR is **logged** in the sensitive-access log (a new kind, "a candidate's interview feedback and scorecards, or a requisition's comparison"), shown by the candidate's number, never a name.
8. **Applicant retention.** When an unsuccessful applicant is anonymised, **the scorecard and panel comments, the chair's recommendation note and the decision note are cleared too**; the scores, the recommendations and the outcome stay.

## Worked example

Requisition REQ-00003 has criteria *Relevant experience, Communication, Reliability*. Three people sit on Ngozi's panel (the chair and two members). One member scores 4, 5, 3 and recommends hire. **Until the others submit**, the other two cannot see it and HR sees "1 of 3 submitted". The second member scores 5, 4, 4 (strong hire); the chair scores 4, 4, 5 (hire): **the last submission closes the panel.** The comparison now shows, for *Relevant experience*, lowest 4, highest 5, average **4.3** ((4+5+4)/3), scored by 3; recommendations: hire 2, strong hire 1. The chair recommends selecting Ngozi, "strongest on reliability and clear communication". The chair **cannot** approve it; HR can. **Only then** can HR raise Ngozi's offer.

## What could go wrong, and what I did not do

- **A new reader of candidate data.** A panel member is a new kind of reader. They reach their own interviews and the comparison for interviews they sat on and nothing else; tests prove a non-member, a member of another company and a member of another panel are refused. **The access right is explicit and narrow.**
- **Narrowing who reads feedback is visible.** The administrators, finance and the auditor could read old interview feedback before this change and **now cannot**. That is your decision of 2026-11-16; say if the auditor should keep access.
- **A delegated HR officer does not hold the new right.** I did not add a delegated function for it; only the HR administrator sets panels and criteria and reads feedback. Say if a delegated officer should.
- **The audit log never carries a score, recommendation or comment while a panel is open** (so the log cannot be read to see a colleague's scorecard early). It carries ids and counts, and, on closing, the outcome counts.
- **Not done:** the rule that a line manager may not sit on the panel of their own internal candidate (internal applications arrive with slice B); the candidate's own data request does **not yet include panel scorecards** about them (a question for the lawyer, written down, not built); scorecards of a **former employee who sat on a panel** are kept as they are.
- **Race on the last submission** is handled by locking the interview while a scorecard is saved, so two simultaneous last submissions cannot each think the other is missing. It is covered by the design, not by a parallel test.
- **Bias.** Scores are advice, shown with their basis and spread, never ordered. Over-reliance on an average remains a human risk.

## Where I departed from, or chose within, the plan (please confirm or correct)

1. **Who approves a selection:** the plan said "the hiring manager's manager, or HR: your choice". I used **anyone holding the existing approval right who is not the chair** (the HR administrator or the Company Administrator).
2. **Closing completes the interview** and sets its completion date, so the existing "needs a completed interview before an offer" rule keeps working; **the old score and recommendation columns stay empty** for a panelled interview.
3. **A member can correct their scorecard until the panel closes**, which includes until the last member submits; after that nothing changes (a correction after seeing others' scores would defeat the blindness).
4. **HR sees only "n of m submitted" while a panel is open**, the same blindness as for members.
5. **The retention anonymiser was extended** (item 8 above). **Retention is on HOLD for the retention *run*;** this is the separate applicant anonymisation that already existed, extended so no new free text is left behind. **Please confirm that is within the hold.**
