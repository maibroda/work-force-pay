# E4c explain-back note: saved blueprints and quotes (built; nothing is sent, no subscription is made)

Written 2026-11-04. For the founder. This is money (a saved quote) and access control (who may see and change blueprints), so it is a critical change under `CLAUDE.md`. **The app sends nothing and makes no subscription: "sent" and "accepted" only record that someone did so.**

1. **What it does.** A salesperson answers about a prospect (the approved questionnaire), and the app works out the modules, proposes the plan with the reason and the runner-up, prices it from the verified price version in force that day, and **saves all of it** as a blueprint. Later you can open it and see exactly what the prospect was told. Pages: Blueprints and quotes (a list), New blueprint (the questionnaire), and a blueprint's own page.
2. **The quote is a snapshot.** What was computed is saved with the blueprint, including the start day of the price version used. A price change later never changes a saved blueprint (tested: a newer version at a very different price leaves the saved quote as it was).
3. **An incomplete quote is saved as incomplete.** If a module in the blueprint has no price yet, the blueprint is saved and its page says "No total" with the reason, rather than refusing or showing a total that leaves the module out.
4. **Linked to the bureau's own pipeline (your ruling of 2026-11-03).** A blueprint can be linked to one of the bureau's leads and to one of its proposals (the proposal must be that lead's). Another bureau's lead or proposal is "not found", with no hint that it exists. The old, never-used link to a company-sales deal is removed; the migration refuses to run if any blueprint held that link, so nothing can be lost silently.
5. **Who sees what.** Platform staff, and sales people and administrators of a bureau. A blueprint belongs to the bureau (or platform organization) of the person who made it, even if they were working inside one of its clients at the time; everyone in that bureau with the permission sees all of its blueprints; another bureau cannot list, read or change them. A sales role in an ordinary company is refused.
6. **Status.** Draft goes to sent or dropped; sent goes to accepted or dropped; accepted and dropped are final. The change is made only if the blueprint is still in the status the person saw, so two people changing it at once cannot both succeed. Every save and status change is in the audit log by ids and plan keys, never by what the prospect is called.
7. **What could go wrong.** (a) A quote given to a prospect cannot be edited afterwards; a mistake is corrected by a new blueprint (the old one can be dropped). (b) "Accepted" does not provision anything: making the subscription from an accepted blueprint is E5c. (c) The blueprint holds the prospect's company name and answers, which is business information, not personal data of individuals; contact details stay on the lead.
8. **Worked example (test amounts).** A ThreeRoots salesperson enters "Lekki Gardens Estate", 120 employees, wants payroll. The app proposes Payroll Core (no add-ons), runner-up Custom with Payroll; the price version in force prices Payroll at 1,500.00 per employee, so 180,000.00 a month, VAT 13,500.00, 193,500.00 in all. It is saved as a draft linked to the lead. The salesperson sends the quote by their own means and marks it sent; later marks it accepted. A new price version the next year does not change this blueprint's quote.
9. **Which tests prove it.** `tests/integration/entitlements-blueprints.test.ts`: who may save and see; what is saved; an incomplete quote saved as such; the refusals leaving nothing behind; the snapshot; the audit entry without the prospect's name; the lead and proposal links and the other bureau's being not found; one bureau not seeing another's; staff working inside a client seeing the bureau's; the status flow; and that accepting makes no subscription.
10. **Honest limits.** I wrote the tests first but ran them only after the code existed, so I did not watch them fail. The three new pages have not been looked at in a browser. The migration's refusal guard (when a blueprint holds the old link) was checked by reading, not exercised with a row.
