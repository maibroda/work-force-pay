# E4b explain-back note: the software price book and the quote (built; no price is in code)

Written 2026-11-03. For the founder. This is money and access control, so it is a critical change under `CLAUDE.md`. **No amount is anywhere in the code: the price book starts empty and every price is yours, entered through the screen.** The app calculates and records prices and never charges anyone.

1. **What it does.** A price book holds dated versions of what each module costs per month. A quote takes a prospect's answers (E4a), works out the modules, and prices them from the verified version in force that day: each module's price times the number of employees or CRM users it is charged by (or flat), summed; the version's minimum monthly fee applies if the sum is below it; VAT is added at the seller's own rate-card rate.
2. **Who does what.** Only platform staff (`SUPER_ADMIN`, who holds `pricebook.manage`) can make, verify or delete a draft of a price book; it is withheld from `COMPANY_ADMIN`, who otherwise holds every permission. Quoting (`blueprint.manage`) is for platform staff and for sales people in a bureau (`BUSINESS_DEV`; a bureau's administrator holds it automatically), and the service checks the organization really is a bureau, so a sales role in an ordinary company is refused.
3. **Two people, and then it is fixed.** A version is a draft until a second person verifies it; the verifier can never be the maker (the service refuses and so does a database constraint). **Once verified, a version and its prices can never be changed or deleted by anyone, the application included** (database triggers refuse). A price change is a new version from a later date. A draft can be edited by deleting and remaking it, or deleted.
4. **Which version a quote uses.** The verified version with the latest start on or before the day. A draft is never used, and before the first version there is none: the quote then says "No price book is in force for this date" and shows no total.
5. **Exact money.** Prices are held as exact decimals and calculated in whole kobo through the shared money utility (`src/lib/money-exact.ts`); there are no floating-point numbers. A price in kobo times a whole number is already exact, so the **only rounding is the VAT line: half up, once.** Worked example (test amounts, not yours): Payroll 1,500.00 per employee for 120 employees is 180,000.00; the minimum 50,000.00 does not apply; VAT at 7.5% is 13,500.00; the total with VAT is 193,500.00. With 20 employees the sum is 30,000.00, so the minimum applies and the quote is 50,000.00 (VAT 3,750.00).
6. **A module with no price shows no total.** If any module in the blueprint has no price in the version, the quote lists which, shows what is priced, and shows no total, rather than a total that quietly leaves something out. A per-user module needs a number of users. A price written for one way of charging is refused if the module is charged another way now, rather than guessed.
7. **What a version stores.** Each price keeps the way it was charged (per employee, per user, flat) as it was when set, so a later change in how a module is charged cannot reinterpret an old price. Saving a quote with a blueprint (so a later price change never rewrites what a client was told) is E4c.
8. **What could go wrong.** (a) A wrong amount entered and verified cannot be edited: add a new version from a later date (and a quote already given keeps the old figures once E4c saves them). (b) Two platform staff are needed; a single-person platform cannot verify its own entry. (c) **Per-employee price bands are not built:** they need a rule only you can give (does the cheaper rate apply only to the units inside a band, or to all units once the count passes the band?).
9. **Which tests prove it.** `tests/unit/entitlements-pricing.test.ts` (the worked quotes above and others, checked by hand; the minimum; rounding; no price, no book, wrong basis, no users; and, for many sets of answers, that the lines add up exactly to the subtotal and the total is the larger of that and the minimum) and `tests/integration/entitlements-price-book.test.ts` (who may do what; every refusal leaves nothing behind; the two-person rule at the service and in the database; a verified version refusing update, delete and a new line; which version is in force on a day; the seller check; the VAT from the seller's own rate card).
10. **Honest limits.** I wrote the tests first but ran them only after the code existed, so I did not watch them fail first (the pure ones passed after one expectation of mine, the order of the lines, was corrected). The screen has not been looked at in a browser. The quote is not yet saved with a blueprint or shown to a salesperson (E4c).
