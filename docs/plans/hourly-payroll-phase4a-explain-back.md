## What it does, step by step

1. **A pure step prepares an hourly person's pay lines** (`buildHourlyEarnings`). It takes the person's APPROVED pieces of shifts (the paid minutes by day and class, from phase 3) and the approved hourly rates (phase 2). For each piece it finds the rate in force on that day (the employee's own, then the contract and job role, then the job role: your H1) and the salary structure for that site and day (supplied by the caller: your H8). It then groups the minutes by site, structure, class of day and rate, and prices each line once: minutes x rate / 60, rounded half up to the kobo **once**.
2. **A piece with no approved rate, or at a site with no salary structure, is not paid and not priced as zero.** It is returned in a separate list with the reason, so the pay run can raise a finding and stop. Every minute asked for is either paid or listed: nothing is lost or counted twice (a test checks this).
3. **The engine takes the lines as an optional `hourly` input** (one entry per site's piece of pay). For each site, the gross is the sum of its lines, all of it (there are no days to prorate), divided into the parts of that site's own salary structure. From there it is exactly the same arithmetic as a monthly person: the parts marked pensionable make the pension base, the taxable parts make the taxable pay, PAYE is the month's regular taxable pay x 12 through the company's verified tax settings and a twelfth of the tax (your H12: the same method), then deductions and net pay.
4. **A person can be monthly for part of a month and hourly for the rest.** The days paid monthly are prorated by the days as ever; the hourly part never changes the divisor of those days; the two grosses are added.
5. **Employer costs** (your H19): ITF and NSITF apply as a percentage of the person's gross, as ever; there is no client billing and no management share for an hourly site, so the costs worked from the client's monthly rate (insurance, uniform and kits, recruitment and training, leave reliever) come to zero; and the outsourcing leave allowance is switched off for an hourly person (see the correction below).
6. **What the record shows:** an hourly-only person has no "monthly gross" (they have no fixed salary: their hourly earnings are their gross); the days worked are the distinct days of hourly work (plus any monthly days); the site worked is listed; the allocation to each site carries its share of gross, employer pension and net pay, adding up to the person's totals; and each site's piece of pay keeps its hourly lines (class, minutes, rate, amount) ready for the stored breakdown of a later piece.
7. **What the engine refuses:** hourly earnings in a supplementary run that pays only adjustments (late hours are their own later piece, your H22), an amount that is not whole kobo or is negative, and a line with no minutes.

## Worked example (invented rates and figures)

A guard works October at one site: 300 weekday hours at 1,000, 60 weekend hours at 1,500 and 10 holiday hours at 2,000: 300,000 + 90,000 + 20,000 = **410,000**. The site's structure is the standard one (Basic 10, Housing 14, Transport 15 pensionable, the rest not). The engine divides 410,000 by those percentages, takes pension on the pensionable 39% (159,900), taxes the month's regular taxable pay (x 12, then a twelfth) and gives the net pay. A monthly person whose gross is also 410,000 gets **exactly the same** components, pension, PAYE and net pay: a test proves it. Add a second site on a different contract with its own structure and each site's gross is divided by its own structure, with the pension base the sum of what each makes pensionable.

## What could go wrong, and what is NOT covered

- **Nothing reads this yet.** The pay run does not collect hours, rates or structures and does not call the step: that is the next piece (with the findings, the stored breakdown and the payslip). Until then no one is paid by the hour.
- **A payroll can differ from a phase 5 report by a kobo** when a person works more than one site on the same contract (payroll rounds each site's line; the reports round per person, contract and month). Both are indicative or exact at their own grain and both are stated.
- **No supplementary run** (late hours, H22), no stored breakdown, no payslip lines, no findings, no minimum-pay warning (H13): later pieces.
- **Component overrides** (a person's own override of a salary part) are passed to the hourly split as they are to a monthly one; whether they should apply to hourly pay has not been decided and no hourly test uses one.
