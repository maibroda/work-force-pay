**What it does, step by step**
1. **Capture.** Someone with the attendance right (operations, or a supervisor for their own sites only) enters a shift for a person who is paid by the hour: the person, the site, the clock-in and the clock-out, in Nigerian time. A person paid monthly is refused (so nothing can be counted twice). The shift is refused inside a pay period that is approved or locked. It is saved as a **draft**.
2. **One person cannot be at work twice.** The same shift entered twice, or any overlap with another live entry of that person, is refused. Shifts that touch end to end are fine; rejected and withdrawn entries block nothing. Two entries made at the same instant are made to queue (a lock on the person), so both cannot pass the check.
3. **A missing clock-out is never paid (H10).** A shift can be saved with no clock-out. It stays a draft, cannot be submitted or approved, and appears in nothing the pay run will read. A supervisor enters the real clock-out later; whoever does that is recorded, and **cannot approve that shift** (they changed its times).
4. **Submit, then the supervisor approves, never the capturer (H9).** Approval is by the site's own supervisor, or someone with the payroll approval right. The person who captured the entry, the person who entered its clock-out, and the employee themself cannot approve it: the service refuses and the database refuses too. A supervisor cannot approve another site's shift.
5. **Breaks come off (H5).** The approver gives the breaks as the clock times they were taken ("13:00-13:30", "23:45-00:15"). The paid pieces are worked out from the shift without its breaks, cut at Nigerian midnight and classed by day (weekday, weekend or holiday, the work location's state deciding which holidays apply, H11), and stored with the paid minutes. Bad breaks (outside the shift, overlapping, or leaving nothing to pay) are refused and nothing is stored.
6. **Corrections, never edits (H16).** An approved entry is never changed. A correction is a new draft pointing at it; the original stays approved and payable until someone other than the correction's capturer approves the correction, which makes the original **superseded** (kept) in the same step. Not possible once the pay period is approved or locked: that goes through a supplementary payroll.
7. **Reject and withdraw.** A submitted entry can be rejected by the approver with a reason; the capturer can withdraw a draft or a submitted entry; an approved one can only be corrected.
8. **What the pay run will read (phase 4):** only the paid pieces of APPROVED entries. A draft, a submitted entry, a rejected, withdrawn or superseded one is never in it.
9. **The database keeps the record.** A time entry is never deleted; a rejected, withdrawn or superseded entry never changes again; an approved entry changes only to become superseded; once an entry leaves draft its times, person and place never change; the breaks and paid pieces are never changed or deleted; and an approved entry cannot have been approved by its capturer or the person who entered its clock-out.

**Where each rule comes from.** H5, H9, H10, H16 and H11 are your answers of 2026-11-19. Two people for a pay-affecting step is the existing rule. No new permission was made: the attendance right captures, the site's supervisor or the payroll approval right approves.

**Worked example** (invented rates, for illustration). An operations officer enters Friday 20 November 19:00 to Saturday 21 November 07:00 at the Lagos site for a guard who is paid by the hour; it is a draft, then submitted. The site's supervisor approves it with the break "02:00-02:30". Saturday 21 November is a Lagos-only holiday. The shift is 12 hours; the break is half an hour; the paid pieces are 5 hours of Friday (weekday), and on the Saturday 2 hours and then 4 hours 30 minutes (holiday): 11 hours 30 minutes in all. At the same Friday-to-Saturday shift at a Kano site, the Saturday is an ordinary weekend: 5 weekday hours and 7 weekend hours. Later it turns out the guard in fact left at 06:00, not 07:00: a correction (19:00 to 06:00) is entered; the original stays approved and payable until someone other than the person who entered the correction approves it, and then the original becomes superseded and only the correction is read.

**What could go wrong, and what is NOT covered**
- **Breaks are times, not minutes (needs your confirmation, H18).** Your answer says the supervisor records break minutes. On a shift over midnight a number of minutes does not say which day, and so which class (weekday, weekend or holiday), the break comes off, and no rule for that was decided. Recording the times makes the deduction exact without inventing a rule. If you prefer minutes, a rule is needed for which day they come off.
- **Nothing uses it yet.** Until phase 4 an hourly person's approved hours are on record but are not paid.
- **Holiday list not confirmed.** An entry is approved even if the year's holiday list was not confirmed; the entry records that it was not (`calendarConfirmed`), and phase 4 should raise a finding.
- **A site with no recognised state** gets national holidays only; the entry records the site state it used (empty) so phase 4 can raise a finding.
- **No limit on shift length** (none was decided): a person approving is the check on an unreasonable shift. The database cannot check that an entry's person is paid by the hour; the service does, at capture.
- **A person moved back to monthly** keeps the approved entries of the period when they were hourly; phase 4 decides what the pay run does when both exist.
- **Time entries are personal data** (when a person worked and where). They are not yet in a person's data copy or in erasure (they hold the person's id and no personal words except the correction reason and rejection note, which are free text). Listed as a finding, not built here.
- **Not built, by design:** clock devices, the pay run reading hours, payslips and reports (phases 4 and 5), overtime and other premiums (phase 6), a limit on shift length, a minimum-hours rule (H7).
