**Nothing in the pay run reads any of this yet.** No payslip, no payroll figure, no payment file changes. It records the agreed facts, with their history, so that phase 4 can use them. Every company starts with hourly pay **switched off**.

**What it does, step by step**
1. **A company switch, off by default.** A company administrator (the settings right) can switch hourly pay on or off. With it off, every hourly action refuses ("Hourly pay is not switched on for this company") and the page only offers the switch. One company's switch never affects another's. Switching is recorded in the audit log by the fact (on or off).
2. **Hourly rates.** A rate has three amounts (weekday, weekend, public holiday, in naira and kobo, each above zero) and says whom it is for: **one employee**, **a contract and job role together**, or **a job role**. A payroll person proposes it with a date and a reason; a **different** person with the approval right (the payroll manager) approves or rejects it. Until approved it counts for nothing. Approving ends the previous rate for the same people the day before the new one starts, and is refused (changing nothing) if a rate already starts on or after that date. Nobody approves their own proposal, nor a rate for their own pay.
3. **Which rate applies** (your rule H1), worked out by a pure rule: the employee's own approved rate in force on the day; if none, the contract-and-job-role rate; if none, the job-role rate; if none, **no rate** (never zero: whoever pays must refuse hours with no rate). Two approved rates in force at the same level on the same day are refused, not guessed.
4. **How a person is paid.** Everyone is paid **monthly** until an approved record says **hourly** from a date (and a later record can say monthly again). It is proposed and approved like a rate (two people), and is refused for a date inside a payroll period that is already approved or locked, both when proposed and when approved.
5. **Holidays limited to states** (your answer H11: the state of the work location decides). A holiday with no states is national. The payroll person can limit a holiday to named states (spellings like "lagos" or "Abuja" are read the way the rest of the application reads them). Changing a holiday's states takes the year's confirmation away, so the payroll manager must confirm the year again. A place with no recognised state gets the national holidays only.
6. **The database keeps the history.** An hourly rate can never be deleted, and once decided nothing about it can change except that a later rate may set its end date, once. A record of how a person is paid can never be deleted or changed once decided. The database itself refuses a rate with the wrong keys for its scope, a negative rate, or an end date before the start.

**Where each rule comes from.** H1, H2, H3, H4, H6 and H11 are your answers of 2026-11-19. Two people for every pay-affecting change, and closing the old rate the day before, are the existing rules for personal pay rates and contract paid-days, reused. No new permission was made: payroll people propose (`payroll.run`), the payroll manager approves (`payrate.approve`), a company administrator switches (`settings.manage`).

**Worked example.** A payroll officer proposes, for the job role "Security Guard", weekday 1,000, weekend 1,500, public holiday 2,000 an hour from 1 January. The payroll manager approves it. In March the client agrees a new weekday rate of 1,200 from 16 March; it is proposed and approved: the January rate now ends on 15 March and the new one starts on 16 March, and both stay on the list. One guard has an agreed personal weekday rate of 1,400 from 1 April: from 1 April that guard's own rate is the one found, whatever the role's rate is. A Lagos-only holiday on 2 November applies to a guard working at a Lagos site and not to one at a Kano site.

**What could go wrong, and what is NOT covered**
- **Nothing uses it yet.** Until phase 4 an hourly person is still paid exactly as before. The page says so.
- **Moving a person to hourly does not end their personal monthly pay rate.** The two records are separate. Phase 4 decides what the pay run does when a person has both; I did not decide it.
- **Contract paid-days and state holidays.** The existing "working days, public holidays excluded" basis of a contract still treats every listed holiday as a holiday, including one limited to a state. Today no company has state limits (the screen is hidden until hourly pay is on), so nothing changes; once a company uses them, a Lagos-only holiday would still remove that day for every contract on that basis. **Needs your decision (H17)**: should that basis consider only national holidays?
- **A site with no recognised state** gets national holidays only, so an hourly worker there could be paid a weekday rate on a state holiday. Phase 4 should raise a finding for hourly staff at a site with no state.
- **A rate of zero is refused** and so is a missing rate (I assumed a zero rate is a mistake). If a class of day should ever be unpaid on purpose, that is a decision for you.
- **Rates in the audit log are kept** (a money trail, as with settlement amounts); the typed reasons and notes are not.
- **Not covered here:** reasons typed on proposals are free text and are not yet part of a person's data copy or erasure (as for personal pay-rate proposals today).
- **Not built, by design:** time entries, clocking, approval of hours, the pay run reading any of this, payslips, reports, overtime and other premiums (phases 3 to 6; your decisions H4, H5, H7 to H10, H12 to H16).
