# Reconsideration of a failed probation: explain-back note

Written 2026-11-17/18 for the founder's approval of 2026-11-17 of `failed-probation-renewal-proposal.md`: **F1** (the supervisor or reporting manager asks), **F2** (14 days and 9 months), **F3** (the same independent-decider rules as today) and **F4** (no change to pay or status) as recommended, and "go, with plain wording flagged for the lawyer" (F5). **This touches no pay and no employee status.** It is behind a company switch that is **off** until a company turns it on. One additive, reversible migration.

## What the code does, step by step

1. **A failed probation stays recorded as failed.** The decision history is never edited. A reconsideration is a **new, separately approved step**; if it succeeds, the probation is **extended with conditions**. It is **never automatic**: no timer, score or rule approves one.
2. **Asking (F1).** Only the employee's **reporting manager** (or **HR, only where the employee has no reporting manager**, and the record says so) can ask, **within the window after the failed decision** (**14 days** unless the company changes it). They write a **justification** (at least 50 characters) and **at least one measurable condition**: what is measured, the target and the day it is reviewed. The extension is a whole number of months, and **the total probation may not exceed the company's ceiling (9 months)**. Nobody else can ask: not another manager, HR for an employee who has a manager, the employee, finance or the auditor.
3. **Limits.** **One at a time**, and **at most one per contract**: a declined one counts as that one; one sent back for more work or withdrawn does not. **A second failure after a reconsideration is final.** It is refused once an **exit has been started**.
4. **HR's review.** A **different HR person** (not the one who recorded the request, where HR did) **forwards it, returns it to the supervisor, or declines it**, with a written reason.
5. **The decision (F3).** A **designated decider** decides: the Company Administrator, or someone the Company Administrator designated (the same mechanism as the probation workflow), **never the person who asked, the HR reviewer or the employee**. **The one who recorded the failure may decide.** The decider approves (a **different period is allowed within the ceiling, with a reason**) or declines with a reason.
6. **It takes effect only when the employee has been formally told (F4).** HR records that the employee was given the written statement. **Only then**, the probation becomes **extended**, the new end date is **the extension counted from that day**, and the probation decision history shows **the failure and the extension side by side**. **Until then nothing changes and the employee sees nothing.** From then on the normal probation process, reminders and decisions apply. **No pay, no pay rate, no payroll record and no employee status is touched.**
7. **The statement (F5).** It is **generated from the facts only**: the earlier decision and its date, the extension and the new review date, the conditions with their review dates, and the employee's right to respond once. It says nothing about pay. **Its wording has not been reviewed by a lawyer** and the screens say so.
8. **The employee's one response**, inside the company's existing response window (7 days from the day they were told); disagreeing needs a sentence. It changes nothing by itself.
9. **Withdrawing.** The supervisor can withdraw their own request while it waits for HR; a designated decider can withdraw one until it takes effect; nobody after.
10. **Closing the gap.** A contract whose probation **failed can no longer be renewed as an ordinary renewal**, whoever asks: that path skipped every control. The renewal form is hidden for it and the server refuses it. The only way back is this reconsideration (where switched on); otherwise the exit process applies.
11. **Who reads it.** The justification and the notes are read by **HR, a designated decider and (only their own request) the supervisor who asked**; **not finance, the auditor, another manager or the employee**. The employee sees **only the statement, the conditions and their own response, once it is in effect**. The audit log carries **ids and the step only**, never a word of any of it. **Erasing a former employee clears all the words** (the justification, the notes, the statement, the response and the conditions); the shape stays. The person's **own data copy** includes the statement, the conditions and their response, once in effect.

## Where each rule comes from

The founder's rule of 2026-11-14 (renewal must never be automatic, with a documented justification, HR review and designated-authority approval, an extension period and revised review date, measurable expectations, formal communication to the employee and a complete audit trail) and the approval of 2026-11-17 of F1 to F4 as recommended. The settings (**switch off, window 14 days, ceiling 9 months, one per contract, 50 characters, one condition**) are company settings in the HR policy.

## Worked example

On **17 November** a guard's probation (3 months) is recorded as **failed**. On **18 November** the guard's supervisor asks for a **3-month** reconsideration: "improved after the warning, and I will mentor them weekly", with the condition "rota gaps per month: no more than one, reviewed 18 December". A different HR person **forwards** it. The Company Administrator **approves** it. **Nothing has changed yet and the guard has seen nothing.** HR **tells the guard** and records it on **20 November**: the probation becomes **extended** to **19 February** (3 months from that day), the history shows "failed" and "extended", the guard sees the statement and the condition, and the guard has 7 days to respond once. **Pay and status are exactly as before.** If the guard fails again, that is **final**: no second reconsideration, and an ordinary renewal is refused.

## What could go wrong, and what I did not do

- **An assumption to confirm:** the extension is counted **from the day HR records that the employee was told**, not from the old end date (which has already passed). Say if you want it counted differently.
- **The statement's wording is not lawyer-reviewed** (F5), and **the ceiling of 9 months** needs a lawyer's confirmation of what the law allows. Both are for your lawyer; the ceiling is a setting.
- **A supervisor cannot open the HR contract page**, so they ask and withdraw from **My team's probation**; HR does the review there.
- **Nothing reminds** the decider or HR that a request is waiting (no digest line, no email), except that a request appears in the decider's **Probation** page and on the contract page. Say if you want a digest line.
- **No read record** is kept of who reads the justification (as for the existing probation reviews).
- **Not built:** reminders, a report of reconsiderations, a second reconsideration, and any pay or status change.
- **A finding, not fixed:** the **existing** probation entries in the audit log (confirm, extend, fail) record the whole contract row, which includes a note line containing the decider's free-text reason. That is the same pattern fixed for recruitment in #219 to #221; I would fix it as a separate small PR if you say so.
- **Not browser-checked** as finance, the auditor or another manager (the tests cover who reads what; no new page was added).
