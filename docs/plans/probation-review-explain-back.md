`# Explain-back note: the probation review decision record
`
`Branch `probation-review`. One migration, `20261201090000_probation_decisions` (additive: one table, one policy column). This changes **who may make a decision about someone's employment, how it is recorded, and what the employee is shown**, so it touches approvals, access control and the audit trail: please read it. Built on your "go" of 2026-11-09 to the plan `docs/plans/probation-review-plan.md`. **You did not answer the plan's seven questions, so I used my recommended answers; they are listed under "Assumptions" below.** The decision itself (confirm, extend, fail) works exactly as before; this adds a record, two rules, a prompt and a line for the employee.
`
`## The rules, step by step
`
`1. **The decision is made as before** by someone with `hr.approve`: Confirm converts a probationary contract to a permanent one from a chosen date; Extend adds 1 to 12 months within the company's longest probation; Fail records the outcome. Extend and Fail need a reason. When the policy requires it, a confirmation needs a signed-off probation appraisal (and any minimum score).
`2. **Every decision is also kept as a record**, in the same step as the change to the contract (both happen or neither does): who decided, when, the reason, the months added or the permanent-from date, and **a snapshot of the signed-off probation appraisal behind it** (its id, its score in hundredths and its recommendation as they were then). A later decision adds a record; none is edited or deleted. If the probation appraisal was not yet signed off, nothing is snapshotted (the decision records "no appraisal behind it").
`3. **A second person decides.** If the contract has a probation appraisal (of any status), **whoever reviewed it or signed it off is refused** ("you reviewed / signed off this probation appraisal: someone else has to make the decision"), even though they hold the right to decide. With no probation appraisal nobody is conflicted.
`4. **A decision against the appraisal's recommendation needs a fuller reason** (at least 20 characters) **and is marked**, but is never blocked. "Against" means: Confirm when the signed-off appraisal recommended a performance plan or further training; Fail when it recommended confirmation, an increment or a promotion; Extend when it recommended confirmation. No recommendation, or no signed-off appraisal, never counts. (These meanings are my assumption: plan question 2.)
`5. **After Fail nothing else happens by itself.** The contract page and the HR digest show "Probation not confirmed: no exit started" while the employee is still active and has no exit under way, with a link to the Onboarding & exit tab. Notice, pay and any contract term are for HR and your adviser; I have put no rule on screen.
`6. **An undecided probation is escalated.** The digest says how many days ago it ended; once it has been past its end for the company's number of days (HR policy, default **7**, **0 = never**) it also appears in the digest's "Waiting for your approval" list.
`7. **The employee sees one line, only after HR says they were told.** HR presses "Mark the employee as told" on the decision; from then on the employee's home page shows one line about their latest told decision ("Probation extended to 30 Nov 2026.", "Probation confirmed: permanent from 3 Mar 2026.", "Probation not confirmed."). **Never the reasons.** Marking is recorded by ids only.
`8. **Personal data.** The decisions and their reasons are in the copy of their data the employee can ask for. When an employee is erased, the reasons are removed (on each decision, and the same words that are written into the contract's free-text notes); the shape (the decision, its dates, who decided) and the audit log stay. **I found that the contract's notes were never cleared on erasure; they now are, with a test.**
`
`## A worked example
`
`Gbenga's three-month probation ends on 31 October. His supervisor reviews the probation appraisal (score 3.80, recommends "Confirm employment") and the company administrator signs it off. On 9 November, nine days after the end, the HR digest says "(9 days ago) — overdue" and, because it has been past 7 days, lists it under Waiting for your approval. **The company administrator who signed the appraisal off tries to confirm him: refused** ("you signed off this probation appraisal"). A second administrator confirms from 1 December with no note needed (in line with the recommendation): the contract becomes permanent from 1 December, and the history shows "Confirmed, 9 Nov, permanent from 1 Dec, probation appraisal 3.80, recommended: Confirm employment". Had the second administrator **failed** him instead, the system would have asked for a reason of at least 20 characters and marked it "against the appraisal's recommendation". HR tells Gbenga in person and presses "Mark the employee as told"; his home page then shows "Probation confirmed: permanent from 01 Dec 2026."
`
`## What could go wrong, and what proves it does not
`
`| Risk | Test |
`| --- | --- |
`| The person who rated or signed off the appraisal also decides | \`probation-decisions.test.ts\` and \`probation.test.ts\` (unit, \`decisionConflict\`): the reviewer and the signer are each refused for confirm, extend and fail; anyone else may decide; no appraisal means no conflict |
`| A decision against the recommendation goes unnoticed, or is blocked | the "against" tests: each pairing (confirm against plan or training, fail against confirm, increment or promotion, extend against confirm) needs the fuller reason and is marked; in-line decisions and no recommendation need nothing |
`| A decision is recorded without the contract changing, or the reverse | each decision and its contract change happen in one transaction; the tests check the contract and the record together, and that a refused decision leaves no record |
`| The history is edited or overwritten | a second decision adds a record (extend then fail leaves two); no function edits or deletes a record |
`| The employee sees reasons, or hears before HR has spoken to them | the employee-view tests: nothing until marked as told; then one line only, never the reason text; only their own; another company cannot mark a decision |
`| A failed probation is forgotten, or an undecided one sits unnoticed | the digest tests: "no exit started" until an exit exists; the days overdue; escalation at the company's number of days, 0 switches it off, a decided probation drops off, another company's never appears |
`| Reasons survive an erasure or are missing from the employee's copy of their data | the privacy tests: the export includes decisions and reasons and no one else's; the erasure removes the reasons and the contract notes, keeps the shape and leaves the audit log untouched |
`| The audit log carries what a reason says | the audit test: marking a decision as told records ids only |
`| The migration loses data | round trip (up, down, up) on a copy of the seeded database: contracts and employees unchanged, schema identical, default of 7 restored |
`
`## Assumptions (my recommended answers to the plan's questions; change any of them)
`
`1. **A second person decides** (not the appraisal's reviewer or approver). 2. **A decision against the recommendation** needs a fuller reason (20 characters) and is marked, **not blocked**, with the meanings in rule 4. 3. **After Fail only a prompt and a link**, no automatic exit, no rule shown (a legal question for your lawyer). 4. **Escalation after 7 days** to the digest's approvals list (it is one list that every HR reader of the digest sees; there is no separate list for the company administrator). 5. **Early confirmation stays allowed.** 6. **The employee sees a line only after HR marks them as told**, never the reasons. 7. **The letter:** a button on the contract page only (it already had one on the profile); no letters for Extend or Fail.
`
`**A change in behaviour you should confirm:** before this, the same person could sign off a probation appraisal and then decide the probation. The existing tests in \`probation-appraisals.test.ts\` did exactly that, so I changed them to use a separate decider (nine calls); I changed no other existing assertion. I also made the digest's overdue line say "(9 days ago) — overdue"; the existing tests that check that line still pass unchanged.
